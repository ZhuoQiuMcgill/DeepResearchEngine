# Statutory Leave Floors Range From Zero to 35 Days

The answer depends on where you work, because statutory minimums range from **no federal entitlement for US private-sector employees** to **35 calendar days at the top tier in Argentina**. Among the major economies reviewed, the most common floors are **four weeks (20 working days on a five-day week)**, set by the EU Working Time Directive and matched in Australia, New Zealand, Italy and the Netherlands. The UK sets **5.6 weeks, or 28 days for a five-day worker**, and Brazil, Spain and the UAE give **30 calendar days**. Part-time workers are usually pro-rated, and the day count is often calendar days in one country and working days in another, so figures are not directly comparable. Employment contracts and collective agreements frequently exceed these floors, and the statutory figures below are the minimum you can rely on. Several rules are in transition, including the UK's enforcement changes, New Zealand's 2028 replacement law and an unresolved Argentine reform question.

## The Americas: a federal gap and service-based ladders

The United States is the outlier. **Federal law sets no minimum paid vacation for private employers** ([DOL, Holiday Pay](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/wages/holiday)). Only federal employees have a statutory schedule: **13, 20 or 26 days a year** depending on service ([FEDweek](https://www.fedweek.com/ask/federal-government-policies/annual-leave-federal-government/)). At the state level, no state mandates paid vacation as such. Only **three states (Maine, Nevada and Illinois) require general paid-leave banks usable for any reason** ([FirstHR](https://firsthr.app/blog/time-tracking/pto-laws-by-state)). These banks accrue about **40 hours a year**, but they can be used for illness or other reasons, so they are not a vacation entitlement in the usual sense. Maine's rule applies to employers with more than 10 employees ([Maine Legislature](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/26/title26sec637-2.html)), and Nevada's applies to employers with 50 or more ([Sixfifty](https://www.sixfifty.com/blog/nevada-paid-sick-leave-laws-and-requirements/)). A US private-sector worker in a state without such a law therefore relies entirely on the employer's policy.

Canada sets its floors by province, so the same job can carry different entitlements. Nearly every province guarantees **at least two weeks**, usually rising to **three weeks after a threshold, commonly five years** ([Employment Hero](https://employmenthero.com/en-ca/resources/vacation-entitlement-by-province/)). The federal rule under the Canada Labour Code is **two weeks after one year, three after five and four after ten** ([LedgerLogic](https://www.ledgerlogic.ca/blog/how-to-calculate-vacation-pay-for-salaried-employees)). One source places the federal three-week step at six years, which conflicts with the more common five-year figure, and the statute text was not confirmed.

Latin America shows the widest range of structures. **Brazil gives 30 calendar days after 12 months**, but unjustified absences reduce this, with tiers falling to 24, 18 and 12 days and nothing above 32 absences ([Marcos Martins](https://marcosmartins.adv.br/en/unjustified-absences-and-their-impact-on-vacation-entitlement/)). **Mexico gives 12 working days after the first year, rising by two days a year to 20** ([IANM](https://ianm.com.mx/ley/ley-federal-del-trabajo/articulo/76)). **Argentina's ladder runs from 14 calendar days up to five years to 35 days beyond 20 years** ([Legales](https://www.legales.com/Tratados/t/tvacacion.html)). Argentina's status needs care: a 2026 labour reform (Ley 27.802) is cited by one source, yet other sources state that Article 150 was not changed, and the Boletín Oficial text was not checked, so the Article 150 scale is the best available reading but remains unresolved. **Colombia gives 15 working days a year**, which can span about 20 calendar days, and Sundays and public holidays are excluded ([Treinta.co](https://treinta.co/blog/calculo-de-vacaciones-en-colombia-lo-que-todo-empleado-debe-saber/)).

## Europe: a four-week floor with national top-ups

The EU sets a common minimum: **every member state must provide at least four weeks of paid annual leave** under Article 7 of Directive 2003/88/EC ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A32003L0088)). Most states sit at or near that floor, and national law determines the rest. The reported ceiling is **Luxembourg at 26 working days**, with **Austria, Denmark, France and Sweden at 25** ([Leaveatlas](https://leaveatlas.com/leave-entitlements)). Euronews, citing 2020 OECD data, reports **18 countries at the 20-day minimum** ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/travel/2024/02/18/want-to-maximise-your-annual-leave-heres-how-to-use-public-holidays-to-your-advantage)). Eurofound reports an average of **24.3 collectively agreed days** across the EU, against 20.9 days in states that joined in 2004 or later ([Eurofound](https://www.eurofound.europa.eu/en/publications/all/working-time-2021-2022)). Sources use different bases, so these numbers are not perfectly comparable.

The five largest economies show how the basis changes the count. **Germany's federal statute sets 24 working days on a six-day basis**, which converts to 20 on a five-day week, and public holidays are excluded from the count ([Gesetze im Internet](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/burlg/__3.html)). **France accrues 2.5 working days per month**, capped at 30 working days, which is 25 on a five-day week ([Travail-emploi](https://travail-emploi.gouv.fr/les-conges-payes); [RH Magazine](https://rhmag.fr/2026/11/09/jours-ouvrables-jours-ouvres-conges-payes/)). **Spain's Article 38 sets a minimum of 30 calendar days**, which cannot be replaced by payment during the contract, and calendar days include public holidays that fall in the leave period ([Concepto Jurídicos](https://www.conceptosjuridicos.com/articulos/estatuto-de-los-trabajadores-articulo-38/)). Sources disagree on whether Spanish part-timers receive the full 30 days or a pro-rated share. **Italy's minimum is four weeks**, with two weeks in the accrual year and two more within 18 months ([Prevenzione Sicurezza](https://www.prevenzionesicurezza.com/public/dlvo66_03.pdf)). **The Netherlands also sets four weeks**, though some sources give 20 working days as a calculated figure.

The **UK sets 5.6 weeks, which is 28 days for a five-day worker**, and this covers agency, irregular-hours and part-year workers ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights)). Regular part-timers receive 5.6 times their weekly days, so three days a week gives 16.8 days ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights)). Irregular-hours workers accrue **12.07% of hours worked** for leave years starting on or after 1 April 2024, subject to the 28-day cap ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/04/07/holiday-entitlement-and-pay)). Bank holidays can count toward the 5.6 weeks, but workers have no right to take them off ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights)). The Fair Work Agency, established on 7 April 2026, is expected to gain enforcement powers over holiday rights ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/04/07/holiday-entitlement-and-pay)).

## Asia-Pacific: four-week floors and tenure ladders

Australia and New Zealand both guarantee **four weeks a year**. In Australia, the National Employment Standards give full-time and part-time employees four weeks, and casual employees receive no annual leave entitlement ([business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/people/employees/employees-pay-leave-and-entitlements)). Part-time employees receive the same minimums pro rata ([Fair Work Ombudsman](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/starting-employment/types-of-employees/part-time-employees)). Cash-out under section 93 of the Fair Work Act requires that the employee keeps at least four weeks' accrued leave and that each cash-out is agreed in writing ([AustLII](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/num_act/fwa2009114/s93.html)). In New Zealand, employees receive at least four weeks after each 12 months, so a five-day worker gets **20 days** and a three-day worker gets **12 days** ([Quotehub](https://www.quotehub.co.nz/guides/leave-entitlements/annual-leave-entitlement-nz)). New Zealand has **12 public holidays**, which are separate from annual holidays ([Employment New Zealand](https://www.employment.govt.nz/assets/uploads/documents/leave-and-holidays/holidays-act-2003-guidance.pdf)). The Employment Leave Act 2026 will replace the Holidays Act on **6 August 2028**, and the Holidays Act still applies until then ([Employment New Zealand](https://www.employment.govt.nz/news-and-updates/employment-leave-act-2026)). Practitioner sources describe the replacement as still a bill, but the official page is preferred.

Japan, China, India, South Korea and Singapore all use length-of-service ladders. **Japan grants 10 days after six months** with at least 80% attendance, rising to **20 days at about 6.5 years** ([TokyoDev](https://www.tokyodev.com/articles/paid-leave-and-vacations-in-japan)). **China's Paid Annual Leave Regulations give 5 days for 1–10 years of cumulative working life, 10 days for 10–20 years and 15 days beyond 20 years**, counting service with all employers ([Lehman Law](https://www.lehmanlaw.com/resource-centre/newsletter/china-law-digest/regulations-on-paid-annual-leave-for-employees.html)). Sources conflict on the compensation for unused days, citing 200% ([Woodburn Global](https://www.woodburnglobal.com/post/annual-leave-and-public-holidays-in-china)) and 300% ([NN Road](https://nnroad.com/compliance/china/)) of daily wage. **India's central Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code**, in force since 21 November 2025, gives **1 day for every 20 days worked after 180 days** in a calendar year ([Offrd](https://www.offrd.co/landing/statutory/leaves/earned-leave-privilege-leave-india.html)). State Shops and Establishments Acts still govern much day-to-day practice, and some give fixed 12–20 days a year. **South Korea gives 15 days after one year** with at least 80% attendance, with one day per month in year one and additional days up to 25 ([Salary in Korea](https://salaryinkorea.com/guide/annual-leave-korea/)). **Singapore's Employment Act sets 7 days in year one, rising by one day a year to 14 from year eight** ([Sage Shield](https://sageshield.com/mom-guidelines-annual-leave/)). Singapore's government said in 2019 that it had no plans to raise the minimum ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2019/0806-written-answer-by-mrs-josephine-teo-minister-for-manpower-to-pq-on-minimum-annual-leave)).

In most of these countries, public holidays sit outside the statutory annual leave count. Japan and China state this directly ([Leaveatlas](https://leaveatlas.com/leave-entitlements/japan); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/-/media/files/special-topics/gtdt/2023/getting-the-deal-through-labour-employment-2023-china.pdf?rev=56f2a35b840d4712a3d5b609ab028c3c&hash=5641B568E030DED0E27B4E08E9C2771E)). South Korea's rule that public holidays are paid for workplaces with five or more employees has inconsistent start dates across sources, so the official text should be checked. No source addressed how holidays are treated in India or Singapore.

## Africa and the Middle East: 21 to 30 days with narrow exceptions

**South Africa's Basic Conditions of Employment Act guarantees 21 consecutive days** of paid leave per annual leave cycle ([GoLegal](https://www.golegal.co.za/annual-leave-bcea/)). By agreement, an alternative accrual of one day per 17 days worked is allowed ([MyWage](https://mywage.co.za/decent-work/leave-and-holiday)). Holidays that fall on working days during leave are not deducted, and an extra day is granted ([Labour Guide SA](https://labourguide.co.za/general/annual-leave-a-comprehensive-guide)). **Nigeria's Labour Act gives 6 working days after 12 months**, or 12 working days for workers under 16 ([Smart SMS Solutions](https://smartsmssolutions.com/resources/blog/ng/ng05-s07-leave-entitlement-rules-nigeria-employers-2026)). The Act does not apply to managerial, administrative, technical or professional staff, whose leave is set by contract ([AnooreHR](https://www.anoorehr.com/blog/nigerian-labour-act-leave-entitlements-2026/)). Nigeria's public holidays, about 11 a year, are paid separately ([Tol Nigeria](https://tolnigeria.com/articles/employee-leave-and-compensation-in-nigeria/)).

**The UAE gives 30 calendar days per year after one year of service**, plus two days a month for service between six months and one year under Article 29 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 ([Payslip.ae](https://payslip.ae/annual-leave); [Qawi](https://qawi.ae/blog/uae-annual-leave-entitlement-rules)). Public holidays are not deducted from annual leave ([Wathim](https://www.wathim.com/blog/uae-annual-leave-encashment-rules)). One source states that DIFC gives 20 working days, so free-zone rules may differ ([RFS HR Consultancy](https://rfsonshr.com/guides/uae-annual-leave-rules/)). **Saudi Arabia gives at least 21 days** a year under Article 109, rising to **30 days after five consecutive years** with one employer ([Wazzifai](https://wazzifai.com/blog/saudi-annual-leave-vacation-pay/)). Leave days are calendar days ([Gloroots](https://www.gloroots.com/leave-policy/saudi-arabia)), and workers cannot waive leave in return for money during service ([Mukafi](https://mukafi.com/en/blog/saudi-annual-leave-rules)). Neither the UAE nor the Saudi official portal was confirmed, so these figures rest on secondary guides.

## Conclusion

The practical answer is a country-specific floor, and the most useful number for most workers in major economies is **20 working days or four weeks**, with 25 to 30 days common in Western Europe, Brazil, Spain, the UAE and the UK's 28-day equivalent. Length of service matters a great deal. Japan, China, Korea, Singapore, Argentina and Saudi Arabia all reward tenure, and Canada and the US federal system do too. Part-time workers are generally pro-rated, though the method differs, and some countries exclude casual or managerial staff entirely. Anyone planning around a specific figure should check whether public holidays fall inside their leave, since the answer varies from country to country.

Several rules are changing or unresolved, so verify before relying on them. The UK's Fair Work Agency will gain enforcement powers, New Zealand's replacement law takes effect in August 2028, and Argentina's 2026 labour reform has conflicting reports on whether it changed the 14-to-35-day scale. Where a statutory figure is the only one you have, treat it as a floor: an employment contract or collective agreement that gives more will generally govern.
