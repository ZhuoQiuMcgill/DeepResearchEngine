# Sources Put the Nile near 6,650 km

The Nile is most commonly reported at **about 6,650 km (4,132 miles)**, a figure used by [Britannica, Nile River](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nile-River) and the [FAO, Country profile](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/040dd3d4-d165-47f6-bbb4-6594ebbb996d/content) for Egypt. Published values range from about 6,650 km to **7,088 km (about 4,404 miles)**, according to [Wikipedia, List of river systems by length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_river_systems_by_length). The spread comes mainly from which headwater authors choose as the farthest source and from how they measure, not from disagreement about where the river ends. The 6,650 km figure is the practical answer for general and reference use. The higher values matter for specialist work, because the peer-reviewed satellite-based study by Liu et al. (2009) gives 7,088 km. Anyone quoting a length should state the source and headwater used, since a single number hides a difference of more than 400 km.

## Published figures differ by source and method

The published values cluster around a small set of figures, each tied to a particular source choice. The table below lists the main values with their stated basis.

| Length | Source | Basis or caveat |
|---|---|---|
| 6,650 km (4,132 mi) | [Britannica, World's longest rivers](https://www.britannica.com/science/worlds-longest-rivers-2225894) | Measured from the headwaters of the most distant source |
| 6,650 km (4,130 mi) | [Wikipedia, List of river systems by length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_river_systems_by_length) | Main table value |
| 6,695 km (about 4,160 mi) | [Paramount Gorilla guide](https://www.paramountgorilla.com/journal/river-nile-guide) | Reported as the Guinness figure; the Nile Basin Initiative figure is reported only through [Egypt Time Travel](https://egypttimetravel.com/how-long-is-the-nile-river/) |
| 6,853 km (4,258 mi) | [EBSCO, Nile River](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/geography-and-cartography/nile-river/) | Measured from Kagera Basin headwaters; the underlying 2007–2008 Brazilian claim was never peer-reviewed |
| 7,088 km (4,404 mi) | [Wikipedia, List of river systems by length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_river_systems_by_length) | Liu et al. (2009) satellite-based study, following the Rukarara route |

The most widely cited figure is 6,650 km, but the sources behind the other values differ in weight. The Liu et al. (2009) study, published in the [Taylor & Francis, Liu et al. 2009](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17538940902746082) journal *International Journal of Digital Earth*, measured along the channel centre line from source to mouth, with stated accuracy of 3 to 5 parts per 1,000. That makes it the most methodologically rigorous value in the set, although its full text could not be checked and its figures come from a search snippet and secondary pages.

## The farthest source drives most of the difference

The main reason the lengths differ is the choice of starting point. [Wikipedia, River source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_source) reports that National Geographic and most atlases use the Kagera, the largest river flowing into Lake Victoria, as the source rather than the lake outlet. Using the outlet would cut the Nile by **over 900 km (560 miles)**, dropping it to fourth or fifth among the world's rivers. The Kagera basin is therefore the accepted basis for the farthest source, and the choice of Kagera over the lake outlet accounts for the gap between the Nile's length and shorter alternatives.

Within the Kagera system, the exact headwater is disputed. [Wikipedia, White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile) notes that sources disagree about which Kagera tributary is longest and so most distant. The Burundian Ruvyironza gives a river distance to the mouth of **6,671 km (4,145 mi)**, according to [Wikipedia, Ruvyironza River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruvyironza_River). The Rwandan Rukarara is presented as the most distant headwater, with an origin elevation of 2,428 m, in [Wikipedia, Rukarara River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rukarara_River). The Nile's infobox also names the Rukarara and gives 7,088 km, in [Wikipedia, Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile). The Rwandan headwater therefore produces the longest modelled figure. A 2006 British–New Zealand expedition said the river was at least 100 km longer than previously thought, based on a Rwandan–Burundian headwater ([Paramount Gorilla guide](https://www.paramountgorilla.com/journal/river-nile-guide)). No peer-reviewed source settles which headwater is ultimate, so the headwater choice remains a matter of judgement.

## Authoritative bodies largely converge on 6,650 km, with gaps

Encyclopaedic and national references mostly converge on 6,650 km. [Britannica, Nile River](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nile-River) gives this figure and names the Kagera River in Burundi as the most distant source. The FAO profile gives the same total length, and [Wikipedia, Geography of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Egypt) also uses 6,650 km. A travel guide reports that about **1,532 km** of the river lies within Egypt, citing an Egyptian government geographic estimate ([Egypt Time Travel](https://egypttimetravel.com/how-long-is-the-nile-river/)), but that government document itself was not located.

Several bodies that might be expected to publish a length have not been confirmed. The USGS figure of 6,650 km appears only in secondary guides, and the Britannica page itself refers to USGS data without giving a length. No UNESCO document with a Nile length was found, and no Egyptian Ministry of Water Resources and Irrigation publication giving a total was located. Because the Britannica length is undated in the retrieved text, its currency cannot be confirmed either. These gaps mean the 6,650 km consensus rests mainly on encyclopaedic and FAO references, not on primary national or agency measurements.

## Conclusion

The Nile is about 6,650 km long in the most common usage, and that figure is the right answer for most general, educational and reference purposes. It is not the only defensible number. Peer-reviewed satellite work gives 7,088 km, and other technical sources cite 6,695 km or 6,853 km. Readers should treat the variation as a choice of farthest source and measurement method, not as an error in any one figure.

For a decision that depends on length, such as a hydrological model, a basin comparison or a ranking of world rivers, the source and headwater should be stated explicitly. The Rukarara route gives the longest value, the Kagera-to-lake-outlet choice shifts the length by more than 900 km, and the Burundian and Rwandan headwaters differ by hundreds of kilometres. Verifying the USGS, Egyptian government and UNESCO positions directly would firm up the consensus, because at present those attributions rest on secondary pages.
