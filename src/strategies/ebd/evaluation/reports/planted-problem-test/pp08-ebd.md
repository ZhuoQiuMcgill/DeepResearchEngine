# Dresden's February 1945 Death Toll Is Most Likely About 25,000

The best-supported answer is that about **25,000 people** died in the Allied raids on Dresden of 13 to 15 February 1945. The most authoritative recent study, the city's Historians' Commission, reported in March 2010 that [up to 25,000 people were killed, fewer than often estimated](https://www.thelocal.de/20100317/25945). Its estimate rests on named and probable deaths in burial, registry and court records, and an April 2010 revision raised its minimum to 22,700 while keeping 25,000 as the maximum ([Wikipedia (de)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftangriffe_auf_Dresden)). Independent historians give higher ranges, such as [25,000 to 40,000 (Frederick Taylor)](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna26977893) and [25,000 to 35,000 (Britannica)](https://www.britannica.com/event/bombing-of-Dresden). Figures of 100,000 to 250,000 were propaganda, and the evidence gives them no documentary basis. The commission's range is the most documented figure available, so this report follows it, but the upper estimates cannot be ruled out, because the commission's full report and the assumptions behind the higher figures are not available in the sources.

## What the terms mean

"Death toll" here means the number of people recorded as killed by the bombing. The 1945 police figures used the term *Gefallene* ("fallen"), and the 1945 estimate covered "Gefallene einschl. Ausländer", that is, the dead including foreigners ([Wikipedia (de)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftangriffe_auf_Dresden)). The "Bombing of Dresden" in the question is the sequence of [four raids between 13 and 15 February 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden). The later raids of 2 March and 17 April are outside this window, and the death toll figures discussed here do not separate them cleanly.

## Death toll estimates and what they rest on

The official police count was the earliest figure. A Berlin Order Police report of 22 March 1945 recorded **18,375** dead as of 10 March, along with 2,212 severely injured and 13,718 lightly injured, and estimated the total at about 25,000 ([Wikipedia (de)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftangriffe_auf_Dresden)). A later report of 3 April counted 22,096 bodies recovered up to 31 March ([Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)). These figures measure the bodies recovered and registered, not a completed census.

The commission confirmed this range from archives. It used burial receipts, cemetery and registry records, and court death-declaration files, and it reported that about 20,100 of its roughly 24,900 named victims died in February 1945 with certainty or probability ([Dresden city, Abschlussbericht](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/stadtarchiv/Historikerkommission_Dresden1945_Abschlussbericht_V1_14a.pdf)). It said that the figures above 25,000 could not be supported by the course of the raids, documents, memories or statistics ([Wikipedia (de)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftangriffe_auf_Dresden)). Sources differ on the report's date: [2010 in most sources](https://www.thelocal.de/20100317/25945), 2008 in [Fox News](https://foxnews.com/world/even-70-years-later-allied-firebombing-of-dresden-still-fresh-in-survivors-mind.amp), and 2015 in one Wikipedia citation. The 2010 date is the most consistent, and it matches the [NBC News report of March 2010](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna26977893).

### The commission against the higher estimates

The commission's supporters argue that the registry-based count is the best evidence, and that the higher figures lack a documented count behind them. The commission's maximum of 25,000 is a ceiling, so any figure above it contradicts the commission's own conclusion. Taylor's 25,000 to 40,000 range and Britannica's 25,000 to 35,000 overlap with the commission at the lower end. The upper bounds in these estimates are not linked to a documented count, and the sources do not set out their assumptions. The gap between the commission's ceiling and Evans's 35,000 is about 10,000 people, which is significant but much smaller than the public debate suggests. The commission's own maximum is an estimate partly based on unrecorded burials, so the true figure within that range is not established.

This report follows the commission's 22,700 to 25,000 range. It is the only estimate in the sources that is tied to a named-victim data set and was produced by a city-commissioned study with access to registries and archives. Taylor and Evans do not give documentary grounds for their upper figures in the sources reviewed, so the evidence does not decide the question conclusively. A reader should treat 25,000 as the best-supported figure and 35,000 to 40,000 as a plausible but less documented upper bound.

## Why the figures differ

The main reason for the wide range is the Nazi propaganda effort. The propaganda ministry promoted figures far above any police count. A 25 February leaflet claimed 200,000 dead, and Swedish papers attributed higher figures to unnamed Berlin sources ([Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)). Taylor argues that the ministry leaked a police report doctored with an extra zero to give 202,040 dead ([Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)). This is Taylor's reading of a leaked document, and the sources reviewed do not reproduce that document. The historian Robert Beck says Goebbels put forward 250,000 dead to exploit outrage over the bombing ([France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20250213-on-the-80th-anniversary-of-the-bombing-of-dresden-tributes-to-victims-are-hijacked-by-far-right)). The commission reportedly found that the Nazis had inflated the toll roughly eightfold ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2024/02/10/germany-far-extreme-right-protests-dresden-bombing-anniversary-world-war-ii/)).

Post-war Western estimates also varied. David Irving's 1963 book gave 135,000 dead, and he lowered his figure to 25,000 in a 1966 letter to The Times ([Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)). Some sources give his figure as 130,000 ([JFeed](https://www.jfeed.com/news-world/srm63i)). Irving's scholarship has been widely rejected. Far-right claims continue: [far-right groups have claimed up to 500,000 dead](https://www.thelocal.de/20100317/25945), and [AfD leader Tino Chrupalla claimed about 100,000 victims in February 2020](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftangriffe_auf_Dresden).

Counting difficulties add to the spread. Refugee numbers in the city are unknown, with estimates ranging from [100,000 to 200,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden). Investigators also had to count bodies that were burned beyond identification, which Evans put at no more than about 100 people in four places ([Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)). Thousands of bodies were found in cellars, and the commission [rejected the idea that large numbers of refugees died uncounted](https://www.jfeed.com/news-world/srm63i). The 1945 figures also count recovered bodies. Some were burned on the Altmarkt, [6,865 on 25 February](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftangriffe_auf_Dresden), so the count does not include a separate tally of later hospital deaths. No source reviewed gives a separate figure for people who died later from injuries. Whether the totals include soldiers, forced labourers and prisoners is also unresolved: the 1945 figure included foreigners, but no source gives a breakdown of the 25,000 by group.

## The February 1945 raids

The raids are well documented in outline. [Four raids took place between 13 and 15 February 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden): RAF night waves on 13/14 February and USAAF daylight raids on 14 and 15 February. Bad weather prevented USAAF operations on 13 February, so RAF Bomber Command flew the first raid alone ([Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)). The second RAF wave dropped more than 1,800 tons between 01:21 and 01:45 on 14 February ([Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)).

Aircraft and tonnage figures conflict. The aggregate is given as [772 RAF heavy bombers and 527 USAAF bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden), with more than 3,900 tons dropped. The [Air Force Historical Support Division](https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/458943/1945-bombings-of-dresden/) gives "nearly 1,300 tons", which does not match the Wikipedia table. Sources also differ on whether Dresden had been bombed before February: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden) records USAAF raids on 7 October 1944 and 16 January 1945 that killed 591 people, while [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/bombing-of-Dresden) says the city had not been previously attacked. The more specific source supports the earlier raids, so this report treats them as having occurred.

The damage was concentrated in the city centre and old town. The bombing and firestorm destroyed [more than 1,600 acres of the city centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden). The RAF's stated aim was to hit the city's communications and prevent its use by advancing forces, while the USAAF's stated target was the rail yards. Historians dispute whether the USAAF's official targets were actually attacked, and the USAAF attacks scattered bombs across a wider area ([Wikipedia (en)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)).

## Conclusion

The answer to the question is about 25,000 deaths, with the commission's range of 22,700 to 25,000 as the best-documented figure. That figure is a ceiling set by registry evidence, not a complete census. The gap to the scholarly upper estimates of 35,000 to 40,000 is real but narrower than public debate suggests, and the evidence does not settle it. Figures of 100,000 and above have no documentary basis in the sources.

Readers should treat the 1945 numbers with care. The police counts were partial and the propaganda figures were deliberately inflated. Any figure should be cited with its date and method. The debate over the upper bound is unlikely to be settled without the commission's full methods and the assumptions behind Taylor's and Evans's figures, which the sources do not provide.
