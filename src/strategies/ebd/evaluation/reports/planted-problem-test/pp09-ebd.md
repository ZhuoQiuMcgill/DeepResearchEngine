# Minimum drinking ages vary by place and drink

The premise that one legal drinking age applies throughout a place does not hold. Canada sets the age at 18 in Alberta, Manitoba and Quebec and at 19 elsewhere ([Alcohol Legal Age Canada](https://legalexpertise.net/alcohol-legal-age-canada/)), and Germany sets 16 for beer and wine but 18 for spirits ([Vertex Legal](https://vertexlegal.org/when-can-you-legally-drink-germany-age-rules-exceptions/)). The United States is split too: 21 applies in the 50 states, while Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands set 18 ([Legal drinking age in the United States, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_drinking_age_in_the_United_States)). Where one national statute sets the age, as in Australia, where 18 applies in every state and territory ([Alcohol laws of Australia, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Australia)), the single-age premise holds. The legal drinking age is the minimum age at which a person may buy or drink alcohol, and WHO-derived tables separate on-premise from off-premise sales ([Britannica ProCon, citing WHO](https://www.britannica.com/procon/drinking-age-debate)). The most common figure is 18, but no source gives a complete, current, country-by-country list checked against primary law. The answer below sets out the ages, the exceptions, and the penalties, with the disputed points marked.

## Most countries set 18, but federal systems split the age

The most common minimum is 18 ([Legal drinking age, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_drinking_age)). WHO data as of 2016 show that 11 of 163 reporting countries had no minimum age, and the other ages ranged from 13 to 25 ([Nature, citing WHO](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-47098-1)). That is the most authoritative global picture found, but it is a decade old.

| Jurisdiction | Minimum age | Source |
|---|---|---|
| United States (50 states) | 21 | [Legal drinking age in the US, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_drinking_age_in_the_United_States) |
| US territories (Puerto Rico, USVI) | 18 | [List of alcohol laws of the US, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_alcohol_laws_of_the_United_States) |
| Canada (Alberta, Manitoba, Quebec) | 18 | [Alcohol Legal Age Canada](https://legalexpertise.net/alcohol-legal-age-canada/) |
| Canada (other provinces and territories) | 19 | [Alcohol Legal Age Canada](https://legalexpertise.net/alcohol-legal-age-canada/) |
| India | 18 to 25, by state | [chartsbin, citing WHO 2009](http://chartsbin.com/view/8ft) |
| Lithuania | 20 | [drinkingagein.com](https://drinkingagein.com/lithuania/) |

Canada is the clearest case of variation within one country. One Canadian source gives Saskatchewan 18 in its table and 19 in its text ([Catalyst Legal](https://catalystlegal.org/legal-drinking-age-canada-province/)), while Minor (law) and Bridge Legal give 19 ([Bridge Legal](https://bridgelegal.org/legal-drinking-age-canada-what-is-minimum-age-drink-province/)). The weight of sources favours 19, but no provincial statute settles the point. Canada has no federally defined age ([Alcoholic drinks in Canada, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drinks_in_Canada)).

Several countries have changed their age recently, and most changes have moved it up. Lithuania raised its age from 18 to 20 with effect from 1 January 2018 ([Institute of Alcohol Studies](https://www.ias.org.uk/2017/06/21/alcohol-advertising-ban-crowns-comprehensive-control-measures-adopted-in-lithuania/)), and its parliament later rejected a proposal to lower it back to 18 ([LRT](https://www.lrt.lt/en/news-in-english/19/1591800/lithuanian-parliament-thwarts-moves-to-loosen-restrictions-on-alcohol-sales)). The Netherlands raised its age for buying and consuming alcohol from 16 to 18 on 1 January 2014 ([Drinking age, Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Legal_drinking_age)). Denmark's purchase age rose from 16 to 18 for drinks between 6% and 16.5% ABV from 1 April 2025 ([Drinking age, Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Legal_drinking_age)). These dates rest on secondary sources, not primary legislation. Latvia's Health Ministry proposes raising the purchasing age from 18 to 20 from 1 January 2028 ([BNN](https://bnn-news.com/social/latvia-could-raise-minimum-alcohol-purchasing-age-to-20-health-ministry-proposes-new-restrictions-wm67kj3)), and that proposal has no legal effect yet.

## Exceptions turn on supervision, meals and drink type

Exceptions are the main source of disagreement. In most places they depend on who supplies the drink, where it is consumed, and what it is. The commonest condition is that a parent or guardian supplies the drink or is present. In Australia, it is not illegal for a person under 18 to drink on private property, but in most states the supplier can break the law unless they are the parent or guardian or have the parent's permission ([Alcohol and Drug Foundation](https://adf.org.au/insights/understanding-secondary-supply/)). Victoria allows a parent or guardian, or a responsible adult with explicit parental permission, to supply alcohol at home ([Better Health Victoria](https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/alcohol-and-the-law)). In the US, Texas allows minors to drink if a parent or guardian is present and provides the alcohol ([Legally Explained](https://legallyexplained.com/where-is-it-legal-to-drink-at-18/)). The count of US states with a parent-present exception is disputed: one source gives about 29 ([Legal Overview](https://legaloverview.com/where-is-it-legal-to-drink-at-18/)), while another gives about 21 in its table, and no official state table was found.

In Germany, 14- and 15-year-olds may drink beer, wine or sparkling wine in public when a parent or guardian gives permission, though never spirits ([Germanpedia](https://germanpedia.com/germany-ends-accompanied-drinking/)). A cabinet reform approved on 12 August 2026 would end this exception and is not yet in force ([Germanpedia](https://germanpedia.com/germany-ends-accompanied-drinking/)). TechTimes describes the reform as abolishing only the 14-year exception, leaving 16 as the general minimum for beer and wine ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324218/20260812/germany-abolishes-supervised-drinking-teens-14-after-science-debunks-safety-myth.htm)). The Bundestag outcome is not reported in the sources.

**Accompanied drinking in licensed premises is the most contested exception.** Where it exists, a meal is usually the operative condition. In England, 16- and 17-year-olds accompanied by an adult may drink, but not buy, beer, wine or cider with a meal, and the exception does not extend to spirits ([Drinkaware](https://www.drinkaware.co.uk/facts/information-about-alcohol/alcohol-and-the-law/the-law-on-alcohol-and-under-18s)). The adult must also be the purchaser ([alcoholissues.co.uk](https://www.alcoholissues.co.uk/alcohol-young-people-uk-law.html)), and the exception does not apply to anyone under 16 ([The Great British Blog](https://thegreatbritishblog.com/drinking-age-in-england/)). Royal Greenwich licensing guidance, however, says it is an offence for an under-18 to knowingly drink on licensed premises ([Royal Greenwich](https://www.royalgreenwich.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-12/Premises_licence_guidance___Children.pdf)). The report follows the meal exception, because four consumer and legal sources agree on its conditions ([Lawble](https://www.lawble.co.uk/children-in-pubs/)). The Royal Greenwich guidance is a general summary that does not clearly address the exception, and the statutory extract on legislation.gov.uk covers only the scope of relevant premises ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/part/7/crossheading/children-and-alcohol)), so the evidence supports the exception without settling its exact wording.

Australia shows a similar split. Wikipedia lists New South Wales and Victorian exceptions for supply with a guardian during a meal ([Alcohol laws of Australia, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Australia)), and the Victorian Department of Education describes the same parent-and-meal condition ([Victorian Department of Education](https://www.education.vic.gov.au/documents/school/teachers/health/factsheet22011.pdf)). The Alcohol and Drug Foundation says bar staff may not serve under-18s ([Alcohol and Drug Foundation](https://adf.org.au/reducing-risk/alcohol/alcohol-guidelines/information-for-parents/)), and Western Australia's youth legal service says under-18s may not drink on licensed premises even with a parent ([YLA WA](https://yla.org.au/wa/topics/teen-issues/alcohol/)). The report follows the named state exceptions for NSW and Victoria, because they rest on specific state rules, while the general and WA statements may describe a different state's law. Canada is also disputed: Ontario Bakery says minors may be served alcohol with a parent at a food-serving establishment ([Ontario Bakery](https://ontario-bakery.com/canada/can-a-minor-drink-alcohol-with-parents-in-canada/)), but Criminal Code Help says that in many provinces parental permission alone does not allow a restaurant or bar to serve a minor ([Criminal Code Help](https://www.criminalcodehelp.ca/offences/youth-offences/alcohol-minors/)). The report follows Criminal Code Help, because it is a legal guide, and the evidence does not decide which provinces allow food-service exceptions.

Religious exceptions are narrower. About 24 US states have a sacramental exception, according to Patheos, which counts 26 states without one ([Patheos](https://www.patheos.com/blogs/geneveith/2023/07/laws-against-minors-receiving-communion-wine/)). Wisconsin's exception covers alcohol consumed in a religious ceremony ([Alcohol laws of Wisconsin, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Wisconsin)), and New South Wales allows minors to consume alcohol for religious purposes such as Holy Communion ([Alcohol laws of Australia, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Australia)). No medical exception was established in any source.

Serving age is often lower than the drinking age. In the US, one source says 41 states and DC allow 18-year-olds to serve alcohol ([Gettips](https://www.gettips.com/blog/age-serve-alcohol)), while another says 26 states allow it ([Oysterlink](https://oysterlink.com/spotlight/legal-age-to-serve-alcohol/)). In the UK, 16- and 17-year-olds who work in a licensed bar, restaurant or shop may serve alcohol if the licence holder or manager approves the sale ([Drinkaware](https://www.drinkaware.co.uk/facts/information-about-alcohol/alcohol-and-the-law/the-law-on-alcohol-and-under-18s)).

## Penalties fall on minors, but sellers face the heaviest fines

Minors face fines in most places where amounts were found, and the amounts vary widely. In US states, Texas allows a fine of up to $500, a 30 to 180 day licence suspension and 8 to 40 hours of community service ([TxDOT](https://www.txdot.gov/safety/driving-laws/impaired-driving/underage-drinking.html)), while Illinois allows a fine of up to $2,500 ([Prevention.org](https://www.prevention.org/alcohol-policy-resource-center/fact-sheets/minor-in-possession-state-statutes/)). In NSW, minors may face $220 to $2,200 for obtaining, consuming or carrying liquor away from licensed premises ([NSW Liquor & Gaming](https://www.liquorandgaming.nsw.gov.au/working-in-the-industry/serving-alcohol-responsibly/managing-under-18s/underage-drinking-laws)). In Manitoba, a minor who possesses or consumes alcohol faces $655.65 ([FedLaws](https://fedlaws.org/at-what-age-can-you-drink-in-canada-18-or-19-by-province/)).

Canada conflicts on whether under-age possession is criminal. FedLaws says it is a provincial offence, so a ticket creates no criminal record ([FedLaws](https://fedlaws.org/at-what-age-can-you-drink-in-canada-18-or-19-by-province/)), while Touristsecrets says minors face criminal charges ([Touristsecrets](https://www.touristsecrets.com/destinations/legal-drinking-age-in-canada/)). The report follows the provincial description, which comes from a more specific source, but no provincial statute was consulted. Germany is another gap: Wikipedia states that minors themselves can never be prosecuted for under-age consumption ([Alcohol laws in Germany, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_in_Germany)). No official German text confirms this.

Sellers and adult suppliers face heavier penalties. In New South Wales, a supplier faces an on-the-spot penalty of $1,100, or court fines of up to $11,000 and/or 12 months' imprisonment, and a parent or responsible adult who lets a minor drink in a hotel or club faces $330 on the spot or $3,300 in court ([NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/liquor-and-gaming/liquor-licensing/licence-responsibilities/underage-drinking-laws)). In Queensland, a licensee can be fined up to $86,350 for supplying alcohol to a minor on licensed premises, and a non-parent who supplies a minor without responsible supervision in a private place can face up to $13,816 ([Queensland Government](https://www.qld.gov.au/law/crime-and-police/types-of-crime/supply-alcohol-under-18s)). In England and Wales, selling to under-18s is an offence under section 146 of the Licensing Act 2003 ([Recording Law](https://www.recordinglaw.com/united-kingdom/criminal-law/drinking-age/)). Persistent sales carry a maximum fine that conflicts across sources: Adur & Worthing Council gives £20,000 ([Adur & Worthing Council](https://www.adur-worthing.gov.uk/licensing-and-permits/licensing-act-2003/children/)), while the statute extract gives £10,000 for section 147A ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/part/7/crossheading/children-and-alcohol/2010-01-29?timeline=true&view=plain)). The council figure is more likely to reflect current law, but no current official text confirmed it. Canada says it is illegal across the country to buy or supply alcohol for a minor, though penalties differ by province ([Criminal Code Help](https://www.criminalcodehelp.ca/offences/youth-offences/alcohol-minors/)). In the US, Washington makes providing alcohol to a minor a gross misdemeanour, punishable by up to $5,000 and a year in jail ([Minor in Possession, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_in_Possession)). The federal 1984 Act required states to set 21 as the minimum purchase and public possession age ([NHTSA fact sheet](https://www.nhtsa.gov/sites/nhtsa.gov/files/public_app7.pdf)), but it carries no federal penalty for suppliers.

## Proof of age and compliance data reveal weak spots

Proof of age rules are strict in England and Wales. Digital proof of age is not legally accepted when buying alcohol ([RASG FAQs](https://rasg.org.uk/challenge-25/faqs/)), and the legal requirement is a holographic or ultraviolet feature, which digital ID cannot provide ([RASG Training Portal](https://rasg.org.uk/challenge-25/challenge-25-training-portal/)). Challenge 25 is a voluntary scheme and not a legal requirement, although licensed premises must have a proof-of-age policy ([RASG FAQs](https://rasg.org.uk/challenge-25/faqs/)). In the US, all 50 states and DC prohibit under-21s from using false ID to obtain alcohol ([Responsibility.org](https://www.responsibility.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Fake-ID-Laws-2020.pdf)).

Compliance data are best documented for the US. A systematic review of eight high-quality studies found that compliance checks reduced sales to under-age buyers by an average of 42% ([NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/book/countermeasures-that-work/alcohol-impaired-driving/countermeasures/enforcement/alcohol-vendor)). Oklahoma's 2023 study of 799 checks found a 73.7% overall compliance rate, with bars and restaurants at 59% and grocery and convenience stores at 83.6% ([ODMHSAS](https://oklahoma.gov/odmhsas/about/public-information/press-releases-and-other-news/2023/odmhsas-and-oklahoma-able-commission-release-underage-access-to-.html)). A Hawaii study of 402 outlets (April 2012 to March 2013) found 84% compliance ([Drugs: Education, Prevention and Policy](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09687637.2016.1197182)). Outside the US, a Dutch test-purchase study found that 81.3% of stores sold to a test buyer (165 of 203), against 34 to 39% in the US studies it cites ([The DRAM](https://basisonline.org/2013/09/25/the-dram-vol-910-if-at-first-you-dont-succeed-try-try-again-alcohol-outlet-compliance-rate-does-not/)). A German test-purchase study found a 49% denial rate for alcohol across 117 outlets ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295100748_Compliance_with_the_legal_age_limits_for_alcohol_tobacco_and_gambling_-_A_comparative_study_on_test_purchasing_in_retail_outlets)). A 2023 vendor study reported 100% compliance with pre-purchase age-verification software ([Vinoshipper](https://assets.vinoshipper.com/2023-Zerolink-Age-Verification-Study.pdf)), but the author sells that software, so the finding is not independent.

The studies differ in methods and dates, so cross-country comparison is unreliable. No national enforcement rate was found for any country. The most recent data point is Oklahoma's 2023 study, and most other figures date from 2012 to 2013 or are undated.

## Conclusion

The legal drinking age is 18 in many countries, but the premise of one age across a whole place is wrong in federal and multi-regime systems such as Canada, the US, Germany and India. Anyone relying on the age for a decision needs to check the jurisdiction, the drink type and the setting. Exceptions are where the uncertainty concentrates. The sharpest disputes, over accompanied drinking in licensed premises in the UK, Australia and Canada, and over German parental permission, cannot be settled from the sources, and the Germany reform and the UK exception should be checked against current primary legislation.

For practical purposes, the penalties are clearest for sellers and suppliers, who face fines in the thousands in NSW, Queensland and England. For minors, the penalties are usually fines and sometimes licence sanctions, though whether they are criminal varies by country. The enforcement evidence shows that ID checks are associated with fewer sales to minors, but no current national enforcement data exist for any country, and the most detailed figures come from the US and are several years old.
