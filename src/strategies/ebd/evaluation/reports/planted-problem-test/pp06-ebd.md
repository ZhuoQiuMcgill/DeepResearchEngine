# There Are 193 UN Member States, but "Country" Has No Single Count

The most common answer is **193 countries**, the number of United Nations member states, and it has been stable since **South Sudan was admitted on 14 July 2011** ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2011/07/381552)). Secondary reference sources agree on this figure ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_United_Nations); [Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/united-nations/)), though no UN-published page found states the total in plain text. Other definitions give different answers. Adding the two UN non-member observer states gives **195**, and US and CIA reference lists give figures from about **195 to 254** depending on whether they count territories. Including disputed or partly recognised entities such as Taiwan, Kosovo and Western Sahara pushes some secondary totals to about **200**. The honest answer is that 193 is the standard count, and any higher number depends on how the question defines a country.

## What the main terms mean

A **UN member state** is a country admitted to the United Nations, with equal representation in the General Assembly; the UN also invites non-member **observer states** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_states_of_the_United_Nations)). **Recognition** is a separate matter: a state can be widely recognised by other governments without UN membership, and some entities are recognised only partly, which is why the count depends on definition ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition)).

## The 193 figure rests on UN membership

The UN's membership figure is the most defensible headline number because it follows a formal admission process. **South Sudan became the 193rd member on 14 July 2011**, admitted by acclamation after a Security Council recommendation the day before ([UN Press, GA/11114](https://press.un.org/en/2011/ga11114.doc.htm)). It was the first admission since **Montenegro in 2006** ([Radio Vaticana](https://www.archivioradiovaticana.va/storico/2011/07/15/south_sudan_admitted_to_un_as_193rd_member_/in2-504827)). Taiwan and Kosovo are excluded: China and Russia block them, according to [RFE/RL via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2011/07/mil-110714-rferl06.htm). One Wikipedia article dates the 193 figure to 17 September 2026, but this appears to reflect a page update rather than a new admission, because no source found reports a change after 2011.

## Reference sources give different totals

Government and reference lists diverge most. The **US State Department** list is reported as **197 "total countries"** plus 64 dependencies and sovereign areas ([USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/articles/which-countries-does-the-us-recognize/)). Two blogs give **195** for the same list, and [PressDeskly](https://pressdeskly.com/blog/how-many-countries-in-the-world/) says it includes the Holy See and Palestine. The State Department's own list was not retrieved, so this 197-versus-195 discrepancy is unresolved. The **CIA World Factbook** is less consistent. Its 2023 archived page says there are **197 countries recognized by the United States** plus **69 dependent areas and other entities** ([CIA World Factbook 2023](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/about/archives/2023/countries/world/)). Its current country index is headed **"254 Countries"** ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/countries/)), but that total includes territories and is not a count of sovereign states.

| Definition | Count | Source |
|---|---|---|
| UN member states | 193 | [Worldometer](https://www.worldometers.info/united-nations/) |
| UN members plus two observers | 195 | [UN Research Guide](https://research.un.org/en/unmembers/observers) |
| US State Department list | 197 (195 in some blogs) | [USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/articles/which-countries-does-the-us-recognize/) |
| CIA Factbook, current index | 254 entities | [CIA World Factbook](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/countries/) |

## Observers and disputed entities add up to 200

The two UN observer states are the **Holy See** (permanent observer since 6 April 1964) and **the State of Palestine**. Palestine was upgraded by **General Assembly resolution 67/19 on 29 November 2012, by 138 votes to 9 with 41 abstentions**, but it has no right to vote or to stand for UN organs ([WorldAtlas](https://www.worldatlas.com/politics/which-countries-are-not-members-of-the-united-nations.html); [CRS, R48306](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48306)). Whether the Holy See counts as a state is itself a judgement call, since it covers both Vatican City and a sovereign entity ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_status)).

Secondary lists go further. One lists **195**, then **197** with US-recognised states, **198** with Taiwan, **199** with Western Sahara, and **200** as a "maximum" ([Facts Institute](https://factsinstitute.com/countries/how-many-countries-are-there/)). Another says "as many as **205**" depending on definition ([TheTravel.com](https://www.thetravel.com/how-many-countries-are-there-in-the-world/)). Adding Taiwan, Kosovo, Western Sahara, the Cook Islands and Niue to the 195 total yields about 200, though that arithmetic is ours, not a published total, and it assumes the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR) stands for Western Sahara.

## Recognition counts for contested entities are unreliable

Recognition figures for the disputed entities are the weakest evidence in this picture. **Taiwan** has **12 formal diplomatic allies** according to Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, which Reuters reported in May 2026 ([Fatuma's Voice](https://fatumasvoice.org/365607/)), although an older source gives 13 ([Statista](https://statista.com/chart/29371/un-partial-recognitions)). **Kosovo** is recognised by **110 UN members** according to Wikipedia ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition)), but Kosovo's foreign ministry claims **115**, and Serbia says the number is in the low 90s ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/katharinabuchholz/2023/02/17/kosovo--beyond-where-the-un-disagrees-on-recognition-infographic/)). For the **SADR**, one journal article reports **84 recognitions with 34 later withdrawn** ([Amelica](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/463/4632029003/html/index.html)), while an earlier legal analysis says **fewer than 50 UN members** recognise it ([Manby](http://citizenshiprightsafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Manby_WesternSahara_JIANL_Feb2020.pdf)). The **Cook Islands** have diplomatic relations with at least **65 UN members** (May 2025) and **Niue** with at least **28** (August 2024), but these are relations counts, not formal recognition counts ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition)). No official source gives an agreed recognition total for any of these entities.

## Conclusion

For most purposes, the answer to "how many countries are there?" is **193**, the number of UN member states, and that figure is consistent across the sources found. Its main weakness is that it rests on secondary sources, since no UN page found states the total directly. Anyone who needs a different figure should state the definition first. The US list gives 195 to 197, the CIA index gives 254 entities including territories, and totals reaching 200 or more combine observers and partly recognised entities.

The disputed cases matter most for policy and reporting. Taiwan, Kosovo, Western Sahara, the Cook Islands and Niue cannot be settled by a single number, because their status depends on whose recognition counts. Writers should avoid presenting a total that combines them without noting that each figure rests on a different date, method and set of claims.
