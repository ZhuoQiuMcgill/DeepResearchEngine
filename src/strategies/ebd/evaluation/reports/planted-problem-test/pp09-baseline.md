# Most Countries Set 18, With Wide Exceptions

The most common legal age to buy alcohol is 18, but the answer depends heavily on jurisdiction. The United States sets 21 for purchase in all 50 states and DC, and Guam and American Samoa match that figure, while Puerto Rico and the US Virgin Islands use 18 ([Wikipedia, List of alcohol laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_alcohol_laws_of_the_United_States); [Frommers](https://www.frommers.com/destinations/virgin-islands/planning-a-trip/fast-facts)). Canada has no national age: **Alberta, Manitoba and Quebec use 18 and the other provinces and territories use 19** ([Wikipedia, Alcoholic drinks in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drinks_in_Canada)). Across Europe, 18 is the usual purchase age, but Germany, Austria and several other states allow 16 for beer and wine. Japan and Thailand set 20, South Korea sets 19, and Indonesia and the Gulf states mostly set 21. Several places have no statutory drinking age for private consumption, including New Zealand, Hong Kong and mainland China. Many figures for Latin America, the Caribbean, Africa and Asia rest on aggregator websites rather than statutes, so the answer for any single country should be checked against its current law before it is relied on.

## The United States sets 21, but private settings differ

The federal government does not set a drinking age directly. Under **23 U.S.C. 158, states that allow purchase or public possession by people under 21 lose part of their highway funding**, and the penalty was lowered from 10% to **8% from fiscal year 2012** ([NIAAA](https://alcoholpolicy.niaaa.nih.gov/the-1984-national-minimum-drinking-age-act); [Wikipedia, National Minimum Drinking Age Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Minimum_Drinking_Age_Act)). By 1988 all 50 states had adopted 21 ([Britannica ProCon](https://www.britannica.com/procon/drinking-age-debate)). Because the federal law does not restrict private possession or consumption, many states allow some under-21 drinking, usually with a parent, guardian or spouse present, for religious or medical reasons, or in a private home ([Wikipedia, List of alcohol laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_alcohol_laws_of_the_United_States)). Texas allows minors to drink in the "visible presence" of a parent, guardian or adult spouse, and Ohio allows under-21 drinking under a parent's or spouse's supervision ([Wikipedia, List of alcohol laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_alcohol_laws_of_the_United_States)). Military installations set their own drinking age on base ([Wikipedia, List of alcohol laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_alcohol_laws_of_the_United_States)).

Sources disagree on the details of several states. WorldPopulationReview lists Idaho with family and location exceptions, while an advocacy analysis describes Idaho as banning all under-21 consumption ([NYRA](https://www.youthrights.org/issues/drinking-age/legislative-analysis/); [WorldPopulationReview](https://worldpopulationreview.com/state-rankings/drinking-age-by-state)). The same pattern applies to Arizona, Tennessee, Florida and Missouri. The state-level exception lists in this review come from a Wikipedia compilation, and they should be checked against each state's statute.

The territories are not bound by the federal rule, and the sources give the following picture:

The 18 figure from the tourism site and the Wikipedia list is the better-supported answer. The American Samoa figure rests on an older news article and a 2026 travel guide, so it is less certain.

## Canada and the Americas set 18 to 19, with some outliers

Canada sets its age provincially. **Alberta, Manitoba and Quebec use 18, and the other ten provinces and territories use 19** ([Recording Law](https://www.recordinglaw.com/canada/criminal-law/legal-drinking-age-canada/); [Legal Expertise](https://legalexpertise.net/alcohol-legal-age-canada/)). Ontario's 19 is confirmed by its regulator, which says **the legal drinking age for liquor in licensed establishments is 19** and that staff must check ID for anyone who appears under 19 ([AGCO](https://www.agco.ca/en/alcohol/information-sheet-legal-drinking-age-and-photo-id)). The Alberta figure is supported by the text of its statute. The ages for the other provinces and territories come from aggregator tables such as [Parties365](https://parties365.com/age-for-drinking-in-canada/) and [Bridge Legal](https://bridgelegal.org/legal-drinking-age-canada-what-is-minimum-age-drink-province/), and one Alberta page that gives Manitoba, Ontario and Quebec at 19 appears to be in error. No 2025–2026 change to any provincial age was reported.

Latin America mostly sets **18**, according to an aggregator database that lists Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru and most other countries at 18 ([Drinking Age In](https://drinkingagein.com/)). Paraguay is the verified outlier. Its **Law 1642/2000 bans selling or giving free alcohol at public premises to anyone under 20**, and the law was published on 22 December 2000 ([Biblioteca de Leyes Paraguayas](https://www.bacn.gov.py/leyes-paraguayas/5076/prohibe-la-venta-de-bebidas-alcoholicas-a-menores-de-edad-y-prohibe-su-consumo-en-la-via-publica)). Mexico's General Health Law bars supplying alcohol to minors but the excerpt retrieved gives no number ([Justia Mexico](https://mexico.justia.com/federales/leyes/ley-general-de-salud/titulo-decimo-segundo/capitulo-iii/)). For most of the region, private drinking is marked "not regulated," which suggests that many states regulate sales only, though this has not been confirmed from primary law.

The Caribbean is more varied. A 2024 travel guide says 18 applies to nearly all Caribbean countries, with exceptions ([Leisure Trip Guide](https://leisuretripguide.com/drinking-age-caribbean/)). Cruise-guide sites place Antigua and Barbuda, Dominica, Grenada, Saint Lucia, St Vincent and the Grenadines, and the British Virgin Islands at 16 ([Florida Sun Adventures](https://floridasunadventures.com/drinking-age-in-the-caribbean/)). Cuba is listed at 16 by some travel sites and at 18 by an aggregator, and Haiti's sources range from "no minimum" to 16 to 18 ([Hotel Havana Error](https://hotelhavanaerror.com/what-is-the-legal-drinking-age-in-cuba-the-complete-honest-answer/); [Tripbase](https://www.tripbase.com/drug-laws/haiti/alcohol/)). These conflicts remain unresolved. Cruise lines often apply 21 onboard and on private destinations such as Perfect Day at CocoCay, regardless of the host country's age ([Florida Sun Adventures](https://floridasunadventures.com/legal-drinking-age-on-a-cruise-ship/)), so a visitor's effective limit can be stricter than local law.

## Europe mostly sets 18, with beer and wine exceptions

Across Europe, the aggregator dataset lists **18 as the most common purchase and drinking age**, including for Russia, Turkey, Ukraine, Serbia, Albania and most of the Caucasus ([Drinking Ages in Europe](https://drinkingagein.com/europe/)). The exceptions cluster in four groups. Germany, Austria, Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, San Marino and Portugal allow **16 for beer and wine**, with 18 for spirits in most cases ([Brilliant Maps](https://brilliantmaps.com/drinking-age-europe/)). Malta has used 17 since 2009, when it was raised from 16 ([The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/07/every-countrys-legal-drinking-age-from-13-to-21-years-old/)). Iceland sets 20 for all alcohol. In the Nordics, Sweden sets **20 for Systembolaget purchases and 18 for bars and restaurants**, and Norway and Finland set 18 for lower-strength drinks and 20 above 22% ABV ([Wikipedia, Alcoholic drinks in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drinks_in_Sweden); [WorldRankd](https://www.worldrankd.com/food-drink/nordic-alcohol-state-control-2026)).

Several figures conflict. Lithuania is listed at 20 after a 2018 increase from 18, but an older AgeFinder entry still lists 18 ([The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2025/07/every-countrys-legal-drinking-age-from-13-to-21-years-old/); [AgeFinder](https://agefinder.org/world-age-culture/drinking-age-by-country/)). Cyprus is listed at 17 by some sources and 18 by the aggregator. Georgia is listed at 16 by a 2021-era page and at 18 by a WHO-derived dataset ([VinePair](https://vinepair.com/articles/legal-drinking-age-world-map/); [Visual Capitalist](https://www.visualcapitalist.com/mapped-the-legal-age-to-buy-beer-around-the-world/)). Denmark and Portugal are also split between sources. Vatican City is listed as "not established."

Germany's rules are the most significant parental-consent case. Under **§9 of the Youth Protection Act (JuSchG), beer and wine may not be sold to or drunk by under-16s in public, but the ban does not apply when a parent or guardian is present** ([Schu-ju.de](https://www.schu-ju.de/jugendschutzgesetz)). Under-14s are excluded from that accompanied drinking, and spirits are restricted to 18. On 12 August 2026 the federal cabinet approved a draft that would remove the accompanied-drinking exception for 14- and 15-year-olds ([News4teachers](https://www.news4teachers.de/2026/08/bundesregierung-streicht-begleitetes-trinken-kein-alkohol-mehr-fuer-14-jaehrige/)). The Bundestag has not yet voted, and the existing exception stays in force until the change takes effect ([LIGA Rheinland-Pfalz](https://www.liga-rlp.de/2026/08/13/begleitetes-trinken-fr-unter-16-jhrige-kurz-vor-dem-aus/)).

The UK sets **18 for purchase in all four nations**. Section 146 of the Licensing Act 2003 makes it an offence to sell alcohol to under-18s in England and Wales ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/17/section/146/data.htm)). In England, Wales and Scotland, **16- and 17-year-olds may drink beer, wine or cider, but not spirits, with a table meal if accompanied by an adult**. Northern Ireland has no equivalent exception ([Innkeepers Insight](https://innkeepersinsight.com/drinking-age-in-britain-rules-and-exceptions/); [Innkeepers Insight](https://innkeepersinsight.com/legal-drinking-age-in-britain-whats-allowed/)). England and Wales have no minimum age for drinking at home, and Scotland has no minimum age for consumption in private settings ([Innkeepers Insight](https://innkeepersinsight.com/what-age-can-you-drink-in-uk/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_licensing_laws_of_the_United_Kingdom)). The Northern Ireland home-consumption age of 14 rests on one secondary source and has not been verified.

## Asia-Pacific ranges from no drinking age to 21

**Japan sets 20**, under the Minor Drinking Prohibition Act, and the age stayed at 20 when adulthood moved to 18 in 2022 ([Study in Japan](https://studyinjapan.org/drinking-age-in-japan/); [Outbound Lynx](https://outboundlynx.com/legal-drinking-age-japan/)). Japanese law has no parental exception, and sources differ on whether minors who drink face penalties. **South Korea sets 19** under its Juvenile Protection Act, and minors may not drink even with parental consent. Only sellers are punished for supplying minors, and providing alcohol to a minor can carry up to two years in prison or a fine of up to 20 million won ([Koreaplug](https://koreaplug.com/korean-drinking-age/); [Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/3287757)). In March 2024 the government moved to relax penalties for sellers who were deceived about a buyer's age ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2024/03/27/south-korea-relaxes-penalties-for-alcohol-sellers-deceived-into-selling-alcohol-to-minors/)).

Mainland China has no minimum drinking age. Article 59 of the Law on the Protection of Minors bans selling alcohol to minors, and tourist guides give 18 for purchase ([You-China](https://www.you-china.com/en/wiki/what-is-the-legal-drinking-age-in-china); [iRoamly](https://www.iroamly.com/china-travel/drinking-age-in-china.html)). Hong Kong sets no drinking age, and since 30 November 2018 selling or supplying alcohol to under-18s in business has been prohibited ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Hong_Kong)). Taiwan is listed at 18 in a single source. Macau was reported to have had no drinking age before 2023, but this has not been confirmed from primary text.

**Thailand sets 20 for sales**, after the Alcoholic Beverage Control Act of 2008 raised it from 18, while the IARD table lists 18 for supply and consumption ([Loopliner](https://loopliner.com/news/southeast-asias-legal-drinking-ages-where-you-can-drink-at-18-and-where-its-21); [IARD](http://iardwebprod.azurewebsites.net/science-resources/detail/minimum-legal-age-limits)). Enforcement is described as inconsistent at beach resorts ([AgeFinder](https://agefinder.org/world-age-culture/drinking-age-by-country/)). Vietnam, the Philippines and Singapore are at 18, while Malaysia and Mongolia are at 21, though sources conflict over whether the Philippines has a single national law. **Indonesia sets 21** nationally, but sale is banned in Banda Aceh and Papua ([IARD](http://iardwebprod.azurewebsites.net/science-resources/detail/minimum-legal-age-limits)).

**Australia sets 18 for purchase in every state and territory**, but states differ on minors drinking at home or in licensed venues. In NSW, under-18s may drink in private premises under parental supervision ([Wikipedia, Alcohol laws of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Australia)). Until September 2018, South Australian licensees could supply minors with a meal if a parent, guardian or spouse was present ([Alcohol laws of Australia explained](https://everything.explained.today/Alcohol_laws_of_Australia/)). Consumer guides contradict each other on private-home drinking, so the state statutes should be checked directly.

**New Zealand sets 18 for purchase on licensed premises but has no minimum age to drink** ([NZ Legislation](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2012/120/en/2017-03-01/); [Alcohol Resources NZ](https://resources.alcohol.org.nz/alcohol-management-laws/nz-alcohol-laws/age-the-law)). Supplying under-18s is illegal unless the supplier is a parent or guardian, has express parental consent, or the young person is married or in a civil union ([NZ Ministry of Justice](https://www.justice.govt.nz/justice-sector-policy/key-initiatives/key-initiatives-archive/sale-and-supply-of-alcohol/key-points-for-the-public/)). Under-18s may drink in a supervised meal area only if a parent provides the drink ([NZ Police](https://www.police.govt.nz/advice/drugs-and-alcohol/alcohol-laws-and-penalties)).

**India has no single national age.** State ages reported across sources range from 18 to 25. Maharashtra is 21 for beer and wine and 25 for spirits under the Bombay Prohibition Act ([Legodesk](https://legodesk.com/legal-drinking-age-in-india/)). Delhi and Chandigarh are listed at 25, and Goa, Himachal Pradesh and Mizoram at 18 or 21, but the blogs and legal-aid pages disagree on Goa, Haryana, Kerala and Karnataka ([Wikipedia, Alcohol laws of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_India)). The state Excise Act is the controlling source, and these figures should not be relied on without checking it.

## The Middle East and Africa mostly set 18, with prohibitions

Most African states set **18 for shops, bars and drinking**, according to the aggregator dataset. Egypt and Ethiopia are exceptions at **21**, and Benin and Togo are listed at 20 and 18/20 ([Drinking Age In](https://drinkingagein.com/)). Ethiopia's parliament passed a bill in 2019 raising the purchase age from 18 to 21, but the enactment status is not confirmed by an official source ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_drinking_age)). Morocco is listed at 16 by the aggregator, but travel guides give 18 ([Lovin Morocco](https://lovinmorocco.com/morocco-alcohol-drinking-guide/)). Several countries, including Sudan, the Central African Republic, Guinea and Sierra Leone, have no statutory minimum in the dataset, though Sudan's status is disputed ([Tripbase](https://www.tripbase.com/drug-laws/sudan/alcohol/)).

In the Gulf, **Oman, Qatar and the UAE set 21**. Oman's age applies to non-Muslims, and alcohol is available only in licensed hotels, restaurants and clubs ([World Travel Guide](https://www.worldtravelguide.net/guides/middle-east/oman/food-and-drink/)). Qatar's guide says parental consent does not lower the age of 21 ([World Law Digest](https://www.worldlawdigest.com/legal-age/drinking/qatar)). The UAE age is 21 for non-Muslims, and Muslims may not drink regardless of nationality ([Emirates Breaking](https://emiratesbreaking.com/guide/uae-alcohol-rules-2026/); [ExpatWoman](https://www.expatwoman.com/ewfood/uae-dining/alcohol-laws-uae-explained-what-and-isnt-allowed)). Bahrain is disputed: some sources give 21 across the board, while others set 18 for drinks under 5% ABV and 21 above ([Global Visa Corp](https://www.globalvisacorp.com/blog/bahrain/bahrain-alcohol-laws)). Jordan, Lebanon, Syria and Israel are at 18.

Alcohol is prohibited or nearly so in several countries. **Kuwait has banned alcohol since 1965**, with no exceptions reported for tourists or non-Muslim residents ([Unearth the Voyage](https://unearththevoyage.com/dry-nations-in-2026-12-countries-that-still-ban-alcohol/)). Saudi Arabia's ban dates from 1952, but a licensed store in Riyadh's Diplomatic Quarter opened in January 2024 for non-Muslim diplomats, and access was later extended to some non-Muslim residents with premium residency ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/saudi-arabia-to-lift-73-year-old-alcohol-ban-by-2026-3225300/); [AP via Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/saudi-arabia-quietly-expands-access-061328091.html)). The government has publicly denied any wider lifting of the ban. Libya, Mauritania, Somalia, Yemen and Iraq are also listed as prohibited, though Iraq's status is disputed: fines are reported, and a November 2024 measure banned alcohol in hotels and clubs ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/middle-east/2023/03/08/christian-politicians-object-to-iraq-alcohol-ban/); [EUAA](https://www.euaa.europa.eu/print/pdf/node/27699)). Iran's sources conflict on whether non-Muslims may buy alcohol ([World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/country-rankings/countries-where-alcohol-is-illegal)).

Non-Muslim exemptions appear in several places. Sudan's 2020 reforms let non-Muslims drink privately, but not in public or in the company of Muslims ([SBS](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/article/breaking-with-hardline-islamist-policies-sudan-scraps-alcohol-ban-for-non-muslims-and-apostasy-law/rpeqpbmvr); [Global Village Space](https://www.globalvillagespace.com/sudan-passes-reforms/)). Tourists may buy and drink in licensed venues in Egypt, where public drinking is prohibited ([Magic Egypt Tours](https://www.magicegypttours.com/blog/can-you-drink-alcohol-in-egypt)). Bahrain allows non-Muslims aged 21 and over to buy from licensed shops for private use ([Tripbase](https://www.tripbase.com/drug-laws/bahrain/alcohol/)). Oman and the UAE issue personal liquor permits to non-Muslim residents aged 21 and over, but tourists cannot get Omani permits ([MyTFC](https://mytfc.com/drinking-and-getting-drunk-oman)). Dubai's tourist licences are free and valid only for non-Muslims aged 21 and over ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/living-in-uae/ask-us/alcohol-law-change-all-you-need-to-know-about-uae-laws-on-liquor-consumption-and-possession-1.1574764296995)). None of these exemptions has been confirmed from a primary licensing text.

## Conclusion

The honest answer to "what is the legal drinking age?" is that it depends on where, what and for whom. The most common purchase age worldwide is 18, but 21 dominates in the United States, the Gulf and Indonesia, and 20 applies in Japan, Thailand and Paraguay. The more important lesson for travellers and researchers is that the purchase age often differs from the consumption age. Germany, the UK and New Zealand allow some minors to drink with a meal or under parental supervision, while Japan, South Korea and Hong Kong focus penalties on sellers and suppliers. Private-home rules and exceptions for non-Muslims are often more permissive than the headline figure suggests, and cruise lines and resorts may apply stricter limits.

The reliability of these figures varies sharply. The US, Canada's Alberta and Ontario, Germany, Paraguay and several Asian statutes are supported by primary or regulator text. Much of the Latin American, Caribbean, African and Middle Eastern material rests on aggregator websites, and conflicts remain for Cuba, Haiti, Bahrain, Iraq, Iran, Lithuania, Cyprus and Morocco. Several rules are changing, including Germany's proposed removal of accompanied drinking and Saudi Arabia's narrow liberalisation, so readers should confirm the current position before relying on any single figure.
