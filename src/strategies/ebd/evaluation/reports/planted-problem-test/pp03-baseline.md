# Vesuvius's AD 79 Eruption Buried Pompeii

Pompeii was not destroyed by Mount Etna. It was buried by Vesuvius during the AD 79 eruption, and the sources agree on that identification. Modern sources name Vesuvius as the 79 CE volcano that [destroyed Pompeii and Herculaneum](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=211020), and Pliny the Younger's letter, which [could not initially identify the mountain](https://www.pompeiin.com/en/Pliny_the_Younger_files/1LetterToTacitus.pdf), was later found to describe Vesuvius. Pompeii lies only about 5 to 8 km from the volcano, so its burial reflects proximity and wind rather than any distant source. The reasons it was buried were its position in the fall corridor to the south-east of the vent, a column tall enough to spread pumice over many hours, and pyroclastic surges that reached the city on the second day. Death came in two phases, first from collapsing roofs under the pumice load and then from surges that killed most of those still outdoors or sheltering in place. The AD 62 earthquake, minor tremors before the eruption, and the slow evacuation also shaped the toll. Exact counts of victims remain uncertain: about 1,150 bodies have been recovered, and the Archaeological Park's estimate is about 2,000. The eruption date is still disputed between August and October. Etna is not part of the story.

## Why Vesuvius, not Etna, is the only supported source

The premise fails at the level of identity and geography. Pompeii is about [8 km from the Vesuvius crater in a straight line](https://pompeiicity.com/en/where-is-pompeii), while a [route site gives the flight distance from Etna to Pompei as 336 km](https://www.distancesfrom.com/distance-from-Mount-Etna-to-Pompei-Naples-Italy/DistanceHistory/16683485.aspx). The only Etna link to Pompeii in the sources is a [fictional character who recognises Vesuvian phenomena from an Etna eruption in Robert Harris's novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii_(novel)). Popular quizzes sometimes list Etna as a candidate, and a [Vedantu quiz marks that option as incorrect](https://www.vedantu.com/question-answer/the-town-of-pompeii-in-italy-is-located-near-class-7-social-science-cbse-608b739263d513071f045a98). A [2026 Il Mattino report](https://www.ilmattino.it/en/the_stone_dilemma_in_pompeii-8967831.html) describes modern sidewalks in Pompeii paved with Etna lava stone, which is a paving choice, not an eruption.

## Wind and column height put Pompeii under the fall

Pompeii's position was the decisive factor. [Prevailing winds at the time blew toward the south-east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum), so volcanic material fell mainly on Pompeii, while Herculaneum, which lies west of the volcano, was only mildly affected in the first phase. Dispersal modelling supports this pattern: the [79 CE tephra is the only Plinian Somma-Vesuvius deposit with its major axis elongated to the south-east](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361075620_The_79_CE_eruption_of_Vesuvius_A_lesson_from_the_past_and_the_need_of_a_multidisciplinary_approach_for_developments_in_volcanology), and its dispersal is compatible with autumn–winter winds. Column height is also contested. Estimates range [from about 14 to 32 km](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/JB093iB12p14817), while [another study gives 31–40 km](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249525802_Temporal_variations_in_column_height_and_magma_discharge_rate_during_the_79_AD_eruption_of_Vesuvius), and popular sources cite roughly 25–33 km. The disagreement matters less than the shared conclusion: a sustained, high column delivered the fall over many hours. The [tephra layer at Pompeii grew by 12 to 15 cm per hour during the Plinian phase](https://www.geo.mtu.edu/volcanoes/boris/mirror/mirrored_html/VESUVIO_79.html), which is why the city was buried rather than merely damaged. Estimates of the pumice thickness also differ: [up to 2.8 m in one study](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/504292), [about 3 m in another](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377027303001471), and [4–6 m in a volcano database](https://volcanodb.com/mount-vesuvius-eruption). The more detailed specialist source is the most reliable of these.

## Pumice fall and surges killed in distinct phases

The eruption had two lethal stages. The first was a [Plinian pumice fall lasting roughly 18–20 hours](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.2307/504292). Roofs and walls failed under the load, and [38% of known victims died in this first phase, mainly from collapsing roofs and walls](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257758316_The_eruption_of_Vesuvius_of_79_AD_and_its_impact_on_human_environment_in_Pompeii). The second stage came when the eruption was [interrupted six times by surges and pyroclastic flows in the following seven hours](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257758316_The_eruption_of_Vesuvius_of_79_AD_and_its_impact_on_human_environment_in_Pompeii), with the [fourth surge reaching Pompeii at about 7 a.m.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225876274_Complex_changes_in_eruption_dynamics_during_the_79_AD_eruption_of_Vesuvius) Surge deaths reflect heat more than crushing force. A [2010 study found dynamic overpressure generally below the human lethal threshold, but the S4 surge's temperature could still be lethal](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0011127). The S4 surge is [described as causing most fatalities at Pompeii, despite a deposit only about 3 cm thick](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2886100/). A [2021 model of the distal Pompeii current](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7925681/) gives a flow duration of 17 minutes, long enough for suspended ash to be lethal to breathe, and the same study notes that survival would have been possible if the current had lasted only a few minutes. Earlier views that victims died of ash suffocation have given way to a view that [intense heat was the main cause of death near Pompeii and Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum). Earthquakes complicate attribution. A [2024 study of two men at the Insula dei Casti Amanti argues that syn-eruptive earthquakes caused wall failures that killed them before the currents arrived](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2024.1386960/text). That result rests on a small set of individuals and has not been tested across the city.

## Warnings were missed, and the death toll is uncertain

The city was already vulnerable. A [powerful earthquake on 5 February 62 AD damaged Pompeii and Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AD_62_Pompeii_earthquake), and [some damage was still unrepaired when Vesuvius erupted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruption_of_Mount_Vesuvius_in_79_AD). [Minor earthquakes were reported in the four days before the eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruption_of_Mount_Vesuvius_in_79_AD), but they were not recognised as warnings. Most people did not flee. [More than half of individuals found in Pompeii died inside their houses](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9135728/), which the authors read as collective unawareness, possibly because tremors were common. The pumice rain probably [slowed or prevented complete evacuation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377027303001471). In Herculaneum, by contrast, [most inhabitants fled even though little ash fell there](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum).

The death toll cannot be stated exactly. Excavation has recovered [1,150 victims, and the study notes that the total is likely higher because parts of the city remain unexcavated](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0377027303001471). The Archaeological Park's e-Journal article estimates [about 2,000 victims in the town, including unexcavated areas, out of at least 20,000 residents](https://pompeiisites.org/e-journal-degli-scavi-di-pompei/il-rinvenimento-di-due-vittime-delleruzione-fuori-porta-stabia-a-pompei-prime-analisi-antropologiche-e-archeologiche/). Population estimates also vary: [11,000–15,000](https://pompeiicity.com/en/history/victims) in one source and [10,000–20,000](https://archaeologymag.com/2024/08/remains-of-two-vesuvius-victims-discovered-in-pompeii/) in another. A [figure of about 16,000 deaths across Pompeii, Herculaneum and surrounding settlements](https://spokenpast.com/articles/mount-vesuvius-eruption-79-ad/) appears in popular sources, but [Visit Vesuvio says nobody knows the exact number](https://www.visitavesuvio.com/en/mount-vesuvius-eruption-79-ad/), so the higher figure should not be used for Pompeii. The date is also contested. The traditional date is [24 August](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/august-24/vesuvius-erupts), but a [charcoal graffito reading 17 October was announced in 2018](https://www.science.org/content/article/ancient-graffiti-shifts-date-pompeii-s-destruction-back-2-months). The [Parco Archeologico later disavowed the October date](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/mount-vesuvius-eruption-pompeii), which leaves August as the position the park currently supports. Neither date changes the identity of the volcano.

## Burial preserved the city until excavation

The ash that killed the city also sealed it. [Heat of about 300 °C killed instantly but did not destroy soft tissue, so victims' bodies were preserved as the ash cooled and hardened](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9142588/). The city then lay largely undisturbed until its [rediscovery in the late 16th century, when Domenico Fontana's canal work cut through the buried city without opening it fully](https://www.unrv.com/articles/how-pompeii-was-rediscovered.php). Herculaneum was found first, [during a well excavation in 1709](https://www.pompeiiperspectives.org/index.php/excavation-history), and Pompeii's [formal digging began in 1748 under Charles III of Bourbon](https://pompeiisites.org/wp-content/uploads/A-Guide-to-the-Pompeii-Excavations-2.pdf). Early work aimed to [find artwork to decorate the new summer palace at Portici](https://www.pompeiiperspectives.org/index.php/excavation-history/1748-1799), and it [caused damage and loss of context](https://www.unrv.com/articles/how-pompeii-was-rediscovered.php). The site was [inscribed by UNESCO in 1997](https://everything-everywhere.com/unesco-world-heritage-site-132-archaeological-areas-of-pompei-herculaneum-and-torre-annunziata/). Reported excavated areas also differ: [44 ha according to UNESCO Italy](https://unesco.cultura.gov.it/en/projects/pompeii-herculaneum-and-torre-annunziata/) and [49 of 66 ha according to the Archaeological Park's guide](https://pompeiisites.org/wp-content/uploads/A-Guide-to-the-Pompeii-Excavations-2.pdf). The park's own current statistics should be checked before a single figure is quoted.

## Conclusion

The answer to the question is that Pompeii was not destroyed by Etna. It was destroyed by Vesuvius in AD 79, and the reasons come down to position, wind, and eruption dynamics. Pompeii lay a few kilometres from the vent and downwind of a high, sustained column, so pumice fell on it for many hours and roofs collapsed under the load. Later surges, which were lethal mainly through heat and inhaled ash, reached the city on the second day. Because the most important mechanisms and the timing are now reasonably well documented, the lesson is that proximity to a known volcano, combined with ignored warning signs, produced most of the human cost.

Several questions remain open. The eruption date is disputed, the column height and pumice thickness vary across studies, and the death toll is an extrapolation rather than a census. Earthquake contributions to collapse deaths rest on small samples, and population figures disagree. These uncertainties do not change the core answer, but they should be stated plainly whenever the numbers are used.
