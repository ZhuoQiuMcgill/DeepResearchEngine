# Historians Settle Dresden's Toll at 25,000

The best-supported answer is **up to 25,000 people**. The Dresden Historians' Commission concluded in its [final report press release of March 2010](https://www.dresden.de/de/rathaus/aktuelles/pressemitteilungen/archiv/2010/03/pm_060.php) that the 13–15 February 1945 raids killed up to 25,000 people, and the city's own 2010 statement confirms that figure. The number matches the German police and city estimates made in March 1945, when [city and police authorities worked from about 25,000 victims](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/presseamt/Erklaerung_Historikerkommission.pdf). Much higher figures have circulated for decades: East Germany's official [35,000](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/presseamt/Erklaerung_Historikerkommission.pdf), David Irving's [135,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Destruction_of_Dresden), and Nazi propaganda's [200,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden). Mainstream historians reject the figures above 40,000 for lack of archival support. The commission's "up to 25,000" is a ceiling derived from records, not a statistical confidence interval, and it does not account for every person. Some English-language summaries give a range of [22,700 to 25,000](https://www.history.com/articles/dresden-bombing-wwii-allies), but that lower bound does not appear in the commission's own press release, so "up to 25,000" is the formulation to use.

## How the commission reached its figure

The commission was set up in 2004 partly to end speculation that right-wing groups were exploiting, and in 2010 it [published its final report after more than five years of research](https://www.thelocal.de/20100317/25945). Its method combined city archives, cemetery and registry records, police reports and witness accounts. The [police counted about 18,300 dead by 10 March 1945](https://dpa-factchecking.com/germany/250213-99-907283/), and the commission's burial-record analysis by Matthias Neutzner covers [about 54,291 information items relating to about 24,900 known individuals](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/stadtarchiv/Historikerkommission_Dresden1945_Bericht_TP1_V1_0.pdf). Of those, about 20,100 were [identified as having died with certainty or high probability in February 1945](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/stadtarchiv/Historikerkommission_Dresden1945_Abschlussbericht_V1_14a.pdf). An [interim figure of about 18,000 confirmed deaths](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna26977893) was reported before the final report.

The commission also argued that the unidentified dead could not run to large numbers. It found that recovery and burial records were [remarkably orderly](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna26977893), and that the [refugee population did not produce large numbers of uncounted deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden). The city's statement says the commission could exclude with certainty that tens of thousands of non-resident victims died, while admitting that the exact number of refugees present [cannot now be determined](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/presseamt/Erklaerung_Historikerkommission.pdf). That residual uncertainty is the real limit on the figure.

## Early counts ranged from police data to propaganda

Early figures diverged sharply, and the differences are instructive. The table below sets out the main estimates and their status.

| Figure | Source and date | Status |
|---|---|---|
| About 25,000 | Dresden city and police, from March 1945 ([city statement](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/presseamt/Erklaerung_Historikerkommission.pdf)) | Basis of the 2010 finding |
| 20,204 recovered by 22 March | Official order of the day TB 47, [per Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden) | Recovery count, not a total |
| 35,000 | East German official figure, set around late 1946 ([city statement](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/presseamt/Erklaerung_Historikerkommission.pdf)) | Rejected by the commission |
| 135,000 | Irving's 1963 book, [per Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Destruction_of_Dresden) | Widely regarded as unsupported |
| 200,000 | Propaganda instruction, March 1945 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden)) | Not based on a count |
| 250,000 | Goebbels's propaganda, per [France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20250213-on-the-80th-anniversary-of-the-bombing-of-dresden-tributes-to-victims-are-hijacked-by-far-right) | Not based on a count |

The 200,000 figure was not an estimate. [Wikipedia reports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden) that the Nazi regime ordered its press to publish it in March 1945, and that it came from a forged version of the casualty report. Historian Frederick Taylor argues that an official police report was leaked to the neutral press "doctored with an extra zero" to reach 202,040. The [Foreign Office also instructed its legation in Bern](https://thestreetjournal.org/fact-check-myths-about-dresden-1945-victim-numbers-debunked/) to use the phrase "more likely 200,000 than 100,000" even though a police report from the same period gave 25,000.

The recovery figures themselves are inconsistent across sources. One source gives [20,096 deaths in a police situation report of 31 March 1945](https://thestreetjournal.org/fact-check-myths-about-dresden-1945-victim-numbers-debunked/), while the commission's report gives [22,096 recovered dead on 31 March](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/stadtarchiv/Historikerkommission_Dresden1945_Abschlussbericht_V1_14a.pdf). Wikipedia dates the 22,096 figure to [3 April 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden). The commission's primary report is the more authoritative source, so the 22,096 figure should be preferred, but the one-digit discrepancy and the date difference remain unresolved.

## Why the higher figures persisted

The 35,000 figure is the most consequential. Its origin lies in a [1946 municipal estimate](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/stadtarchiv/Historikerkommission_Dresden1945_Abschlussbericht_V1_14a.pdf) that rose from the earlier 25,000 without a new count. According to the city's statement, the city [later fixed on 35,000 from 1965](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/presseamt/Erklaerung_Historikerkommission.pdf) and kept it as the official number in East Germany. The commission found that Max Seydewitz's 1955 book and Walter Weidauer's later work [canonised the figure](https://www.dresden.de/media/pdf/stadtarchiv/Historikerkommission_Dresden1945_Abschlussbericht_V1_14a.pdf). The number of people registered as missing was also about 35,000, and [around 10,000 of them were later found alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden). That coincidence may explain why the figure was mistaken for a death toll, although the sources do not prove the link.

Irving's figure rested on a thin base. [Irving's 135,000 came from Hanns Voigt](https://openhistorysociety.org/members-articles/the-dresden-triangle-by-paul-addison), who ran the missing-persons office and had identified about 40,000 victims. Irving later lowered his estimate to [25,000 in a 1966 Times letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden), an admission that undercuts his earlier claims. Taylor and Richard Evans, in his [expert report on Irving](https://fpp.co.uk/documents/expert-report-professor-richard-evans-case-study-c-reichskristallnacht-november-1938/part/37), both argue that the 135,000 figure rests on one source.

Recent far-right claims keep the high numbers in circulation. [Tagesspiegel reports banners claiming 350,000](https://www.tagesspiegel.de/politik/die-ganze-republik-beruht-auf-lugen-der-geschichte-neonazis-marschieren-trotz-blockaden-durch-dresden-15251585.html), and the AfD leader Tino Chrupalla has [assumed about 100,000 deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tino_Chrupalla). These claims do not offer archival alternatives. They rest on refugee estimates and eyewitness memory.

## Where scholarly disagreement remains

Serious historians now broadly accept that the toll cannot be exactly known, but they differ on the range. [Wikipedia summarises that the numbers are "no longer a major point of contention among historians"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden), but individual estimates vary. Taylor gives 25,000 to 40,000, Kershaw 25,000, Evans 35,000 and Max Hastings 30,000 to 100,000 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Dresden); [South African Military History Society](http://samilitaryhistory.org/lectures/dresden.html)). Taylor's range overlaps with the commission's ceiling at the low end, so the difference is mainly about how much weight to give unregistered deaths among refugees, foreign workers and prisoners.

Critiques of the commission are mainly partisan. The [Dresden-Dossier 1945 site argues](http://dresden-dossier1945.de/dilemma.php) that the commission could not prove all bodies were recovered or count the refugees. The [Preußische Allgemeine Zeitung argues](https://paz.de/artikel/im-hoellenfeuer-aus-menschenhand-a13592.html) that the figure mostly reflects burial reports from three cemeteries. Both sites are openly partisan, and neither offers a competing archival count. The strongest mainstream response is that the commission's record-based reconstruction shows the unaccounted "dark figure" could not reach five digits. A 2025 [dpa fact check rated claims of 150,000 to one million as false](https://dpa-factchecking.com/germany/250213-99-907283/).

## Conclusion

The answer to the research question is that about **25,000 people died**, with the commission's formal wording being "up to 25,000". That figure has the strongest support: it matches the police and city figures from March 1945, it is confirmed by the commission's archival work, and it is consistent with the burial and recovery counts. Its main weakness is that it cannot account for every unregistered person, so the true figure may be modestly higher, as Taylor's 25,000 to 40,000 range suggests.

The higher numbers reveal more about politics than about the archive. The 35,000 figure reflects a postwar administrative choice, while the 135,000 and 200,000+ figures rest on a single oral source and a propaganda instruction. Anyone citing the death toll should use "up to 25,000" with the commission's attribution, note that the 35,000 figure was the East German official number, and avoid the propaganda and Irving figures unless they are being discussed as myths.
