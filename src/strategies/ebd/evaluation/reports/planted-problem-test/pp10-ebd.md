# Your Leave Depends on Country and Employment Status

No single number answers this question. Statutory paid annual leave is set by national law, and the figure depends on the country, the employment status, the working pattern and length of service. For a full-time worker on a five-day week, the statutory minimum is **28 days in the UK** ([GOV.UK, Holiday entitlement](https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights)), **20 days at the EU Working Time Directive floor of four weeks** ([OSHA, Directive 2003/88/EC](https://osha.europa.eu/en/legislation/directives/directive-2003-88-ec)), and **20 days in Australia** ([Wikipedia, Annual leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_leave)). Canada's federal rule gives **two weeks after one year**, which is ten working days on a five-day week ([Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/workplace/federal-labour-standards/vacations-holidays.html)). In the United States, **no federal law requires private employers to give paid vacation** ([Justia, Paid Vacation](https://www.justia.com/employment/employee-benefits/time-off-of-work/paid-vacation/)). Status and hours change the figure: UK part-timers receive pro-rated leave, and Australian casual employees receive no NES annual leave ([Fair Work Ombudsman, Annual leave](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/leave/annual-leave)). Contracts can add leave above these floors, but in the UK, EU and Australia they cannot go below them. An exact answer therefore needs the country, the working pattern, the length of service and the contract.

## What the key terms mean and how they are measured

"Paid annual leave" means paid time away from work guaranteed by statute or contract, separate from sick leave. In the UK, almost all workers are legally entitled to **5.6 weeks' paid holiday a year** ([GOV.UK, Holiday entitlement](https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights)). "Statutory minimum" is the floor set by national law, which the EU Directive sets at **at least four weeks** ([OSHA, Directive 2003/88/EC](https://osha.europa.eu/en/legislation/directives/directive-2003-88-ec)). A "day" is not counted the same way everywhere. Germany counts working days from Monday to Saturday ([How-to-Germany](https://www.how-to-germany.com/work/vacation/)), while France's five weeks may be counted as 30 working days or 25 business days ([TrackingTime](https://trackingtime.co/leave-management-software/global-pto-guide.html)), so day totals are not directly comparable.

Most secondary tables list the EU Directive's floor and a few member-state figures. Those figures were not checked against national statutes, so the table relies on the primary or official sources where they exist. One aggregator gives the Directive's minimum as 28 days ([eubiztools](https://eubiztools.com/working-days-calculator/eu-working-time-directive/)), but the Directive text and most sources say four weeks, so the report follows the four-week figure.

The US is the country where the sources disagree most, and three disputes arise there.

The first dispute is whether the client is entitled to paid leave at all. The assumption is supported by statutes in the UK, EU, Australia and Canada, and by Wikipedia's general statement that most countries mandate a minimum by law ([Wikipedia, Annual leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_leave)). The opposing account, from [Justia](https://www.justia.com/employment/employee-benefits/time-off-of-work/paid-vacation/), [Absenca](https://absenca.com/blog/is-pto-required-by-law-us/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_leave) and [Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/List_of_minimum_annual_leave_by_country), is that the US has no federal or state statutory minimum for vacation. This report follows the federal view, because every source that addresses federal law agrees on it, but a US worker may still have paid leave through the employer's contract or state rules.

The second dispute is whether Maine, Nevada and Illinois require paid time off. [Payoneer](https://www.payoneer.com/resources/workforce-management/leave-policy/the-united-states/) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_leave) say these states require paid time off usable for any purpose, though Wikipedia names only Maine and Nevada. [Absenca](https://absenca.com/blog/is-pto-required-by-law-us/) makes the same claim on one page while stating on another that no state requires paid vacation, and Wikiwand's copy of the minimum-leave list records no state minimum at all. The report treats these as general-purpose leave rules that several sources name, but the evidence does not decide whether they amount to an annual-leave minimum, because no official state statute is cited.

The third dispute concerns federal contractors. [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/insights/law-and-policy/state-laws-limit-vacation-forfeitures/) says contracts under the Davis-Bacon and Service Contract Acts must provide minimum fringe benefits, including paid vacation, while [Justia](https://www.justia.com/employment/employee-benefits/time-off-of-work/paid-vacation/) and [Absenca](https://absenca.com/blog/is-pto-required-by-law-us/) say no federal law gives paid vacation to any worker. The report follows the general view, because the Mercer point describes a condition attached to certain federal contracts rather than a right held by all workers. The cited sources do not establish how far that condition extends.

## Status, hours and length of service change the total

In the UK, almost all workers are covered, including agency, zero-hours and irregular-hours workers, but **genuinely self-employed people have no statutory paid holiday** ([LITRG, Holiday pay](https://www.litrg.org.uk/working/employment-status/employment-rights/holiday-pay)). A worker is someone who personally performs the work and is not genuinely self-employed ([Davidson Morris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/holiday-entitlement/)). Misclassified workers may bring claims for unpaid holiday pay ([Davidson Morris](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/holiday-entitlement/)). Regular part-time workers get 5.6 weeks pro rata, so **three days a week gives 16.8 days**, and the total is capped at 28 days, so a six-day week still gives 28 ([GOV.UK, Holiday entitlement](https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights)). For leave years beginning on or after **1 April 2024**, irregular-hours and part-year workers accrue **12.07% of hours worked** ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2024/latest-changes-to-holiday-pay-and-what-they-mean-for-employers)). This method corrects the 2022 Supreme Court ruling in *Harpur Trust v Brazel*, which had let part-year workers receive proportionally more holiday ([Which Payroll](https://whichpayroll.com/uk/uk-holiday-entitlement)). No official source gives a worked example for a variable-hours part-timer, so that calculation should be checked with the GOV.UK calculator.

In Australia, full-time and part-time employees get four weeks, pro rata for part-timers, but **casual employees get no NES annual leave** ([Business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/people/employees/employees-pay-leave-and-entitlements)). A casual employee of 12 months with regular and systematic hours may request conversion to permanent work ([Fair Work Centre](https://www.fairworkcentre.com.au/employer-tips/casual-annual-leave-entitlements-loading-payout/)). Service matters in Canada more than in the UK or Australia. The federal entitlement rises from two to three weeks after five years and to four weeks after ten ([Canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/jobs/workplace/federal-labour-standards/vacations-holidays.html)). Provincial thresholds are disputed. [CXC Global](https://www.cxcglobal.com/global-hiring-guide/canada/leave-time-off-in-canada/) and [Awditify](https://awditify.com/blog/vacation-pay-rules-in-canada-province-by-province-guide-for-employers) both put Quebec's second tier at three years, while [Leave Balance](https://www.leavebalance.com/leave-laws/ca/annual/) puts it at six years, so the majority of sources favour three years, but the writer should check provincial law. In the UK and Australia, accrual starts from the first day of work ([business.gov.au](https://business.gov.au/people/employees/employees-pay-leave-and-entitlements)).

## Contracts and collective agreements set the real figure above the floor

The statutory figures are floors. In the UK, an employer cannot reduce entitlement below 5.6 weeks ([PayFit](https://payfit.com/blog/holiday-entitlement-uk/)), and a contract term offering only a token £1 in lieu of untaken holiday on termination was held impermissible ([Practical Law](https://uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com/1-541-9445?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default))). Leave above 28 days is governed by the contract, which also decides carry-over ([IRIS](https://www.iris.co.uk/glossary/holiday-entitlement/)). The UK written statement must state the holiday entitlement, including whether public holidays are included ([Citation](https://www.citation.co.uk/news/hr-and-employment-law/employment-contract-requirements-uk-how-to-check-your-contracts-are-compliant/)). In Australia, a clause giving less than the NES has no effect to that extent ([Rosterelf](https://www.rosterelf.com/blog/fair-work-employment-contract-requirements)), and the Fair Work Ombudsman says employers can give more than the NES ([Fair Work Ombudsman](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/leave/annual-leave)).

Collective agreements often go well beyond the floor. The NHS Agenda for Change gives **27 days plus eight public holidays on appointment, 29 days after five years, and 33 days after ten years** ([NHS Employers](https://www.nhsemployers.org/system/files/2021-06/TCS-handbook-version-070708.pdf); [RCN](https://www.rcn.org.uk/Get-Help/RCN-advice/annual-leave-and-holiday-pay)). Across the EU, Eurofound reports an **average collectively agreed entitlement of 24.3 days**, with 25.3 days in the EU14 and 20.9 days elsewhere ([Eurofound](https://www.eurofound.europa.eu/en/publications/all/balancing-the-clock-how-europe-works-and-rests)).

In the US, employer policy is the main source of paid vacation. Secondary aggregators say most employers give at least 10 days after one year ([Vacation Tracker](https://vacationtracker.io/leave-laws/usa/)), but these figures are not from a large-employer survey. A US official statistic cited on Wikipedia reports that **80% of private-industry workers had access to paid vacation benefits in March 2026** ([Wikipedia, Annual leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_leave)). That measures access, not the number of days.

## Claiming, enforcing and the limits that apply

To check an entitlement, UK workers should start with their contract and then use the GOV.UK calculator, which Acas and Citizens Advice point to ([Acas](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-holiday-entitlement); [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/work/holidays-and-holiday-pay/check-if-youre-entitled-to-paid-holidays/)). Australian workers can use the Fair Work Ombudsman's Leave Calculator ([Fair Work Ombudsman, Leave](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/leave)), but the award or agreement must be checked because it governs rostering and averaging ([Fair Work Ombudsman, Averaging of hours](https://library.fairwork.gov.au/viewer/?krn=K700454)). The sources do not establish how a payslip shows leave balances.

For UK claims, the route is Acas early conciliation followed by an employment tribunal. For relevant dates on or after **1 October 2026**, the primary deadline has moved from three months to six months less one day ([Acas](https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-tribunal-time-limits); [Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/news/employment-tribunal-claim-deadlines-october-2026/)). Earlier problems still fall under three months ([Acas](https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-rights-act-2025)), and some Acas holiday pages still refer to three months. A tribunal can extend the time limit if bringing the claim in time was not reasonably practicable ([HW Legal](https://www.hwlegal.co.uk/post/employment-tribunal-claim-time-limits-explained)). In Australia, the Fair Work Ombudsman provides free help on underpayments ([Rosterelf](https://www.rosterelf.com/blog/underpaid-at-work-what-to-do-australia)), and unpaid entitlements can generally be recovered within six years ([Clayton Utz](https://www.claytonutz.com/insights/2025/may/the-fair-work-ombudsman-releases-guidance-on-payroll-remediation-programs)). The small-claims threshold is reported as $100,000 ([Zoe Law](https://zoelaw.ai/guides/unpaid-wages-fair-work)), while an older factsheet gives $20,000 ([RLC](https://rlc.org.au/sites/default/files/attachments/Underpayments%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf)), so the newer figure is preferred. No EU complaint route or US or Canadian time limit was identified.

Several limits apply during the year and on leaving. In the UK, employers must give notice at least as long as the leave requested before refusing or cancelling it ([Acas](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-holiday-entitlement/asking-for-and-taking-holiday)), and the default notice for taking leave is twice its length unless the contract says otherwise ([Absenca](https://absenca.com/blog/holiday-during-notice-period-uk/)). Untaken statutory leave is paid on leaving ([Acas](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-holiday-entitlement/asking-for-and-taking-holiday)). The UK carry-over rules are contested: one vendor says Acas describes a maximum of four weeks, while GOV.UK says workers can carry over their whole entitlement ([Calamari](https://www.calamari.io/blog/holiday-carry-over-rules-uk-2026-employer-guide)), so the contract and GOV.UK should be checked directly. In the EU, leave cannot be replaced by money during employment, and leave does not lapse while a worker is sick and unable to take it ([Timesheet.io](https://timesheet.io/en/blog/eu-working-time-directive-explained)). In Australia, leave can be cashed out only where the award allows it, and employees must keep at least four weeks ([Fair Work Ombudsman](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/tools-and-resources/fact-sheets/minimum-workplace-entitlements/annual-leave)). In US states that require payout, the rules are contested: one source counts ten states requiring payout on separation ([FirstHR](https://firsthr.app/blog/time-tracking/pto-laws-by-state)), another counts eleven ([Boostpoint](https://boostpoint.com/pto-payout-laws-by-state/)).

## Conclusion

The honest answer is a range that depends on where the client works. For the UK, EU and Australia, the statutory floor is clear: 28 days for a full-time UK worker on five days, 20 days at the EU floor, and 20 days in Australia for a full-time employee. Canada's federal floor starts lower and rises with service, and the US has no federal number. The biggest risk is a mismatch between the statutory floor and the contract, so the client should compare the two before assuming the statutory figure is their entitlement.

Several points remain unsettled and should be checked against primary sources before anyone relies on them. These include the UK carry-over rules, Canadian provincial thresholds, US state PTO and payout laws, the scope of the federal contractor rule, and the Australian small-claims limit. Until the country, employment status, hours and service are known, the most defensible statement is that the client is entitled to paid annual leave only under the statute that governs their job.
