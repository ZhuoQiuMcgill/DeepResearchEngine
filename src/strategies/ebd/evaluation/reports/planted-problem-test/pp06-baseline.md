# Most Sources Count 193 Countries Today

The most defensible answer is **193 countries**, the number of United Nations member states as of 20 November 2026. The UN count has not changed since **South Sudan joined on 14 July 2011** ([UN, Membership of Principal UN Organs](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/membership-of-principal-un-organs)), and the Wikipedia enlargement article also gives 193 as of 17 September 2026 ([Wikipedia, Enlargement of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_the_United_Nations)). Other common counts are higher because they use different criteria. Adding the two non-member observer states, the Holy See and Palestine, gives **195** ([WION, UNGA 2026](https://www.wionews.com/world/unga-2026-how-many-countries-are-members-of-the-united-nations-full-list-1789550537103)). Wikipedia's sovereign-states list, which also counts de facto and limited-recognition states, gives **205 entities** on its live page ([Wikipedia, List of sovereign states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states)). Standards that include dependent territories go higher still, with **249** entries in ISO 3166-1 ([Wikipedia, ISO 3166-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-1)). The figure you choose depends on whether you count only UN members or also recognised observers, contested states and territories. For most purposes, 193 is the standard answer.

## The UN count is stable and nothing has changed it

The 193 figure has held for more than fifteen years, and no source found reports a new UN admission, dissolution or recognised change of status in 2025 or 2026. The UN's General Assembly membership page lists all 193 members, with South Sudan as the 193rd ([UN, Membership of Principal UN Organs](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/membership-of-principal-un-organs)). The Congressional Research Service also names South Sudan as the most recent addition ([Congressional Research Service, United Nations Membership: In Brief](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48306)). The 2026 Security Council elections changed which members sit on the Council, not the membership total. Five states were elected for 2027–2028: Austria, Portugal, Kyrgyzstan, Zimbabwe and Trinidad and Tobago ([PassBlue](https://passblue.com/2026/06/03/five-new-countries-elected-to-the-un-security-councils-2027-28-term/)).

The main pending case is **Bougainville**, an autonomous region of Papua New Guinea. Its **97.7%** vote for independence in a 2019 non-binding referendum is a political fact, not a legal one ([Reuters via WHBL](https://whbl.com/2026/08/26/png-parliament-to-consider-bougainville-regions-bid-for-independence/)). PNG's parliament was due to consider the result on 27 August 2026, and no vote date was announced ([Reuters via WHBL](https://whbl.com/2026/08/26/png-parliament-to-consider-bougainville-regions-bid-for-independence/)). Bougainville's leaders set **1 September 2030** for full independence, and parliament had not ratified that agreement ([Wikipedia, 2019 Bougainvillean independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bougainvillean_independence_referendum)). The PNG government also said approval would need a **three-quarter majority** ([Seasia.co](https://seasia.co/2026/08/25/indonesia-may-soon-have-a-new-neighbor-bougainville)). The outcome of the parliamentary consideration was not found in the sources. Bougainville therefore does not change the current count.

## Observers and contested states push the count higher

The two non-member observer states are the **Holy See**, which has held observer status since 1964, and **Palestine**, which was upgraded by General Assembly resolution 67/19 on 29 November 2012, by a vote of **138 to 9 with 41 abstentions** ([Wikipedia, Permanent Observer of the Holy See to the UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Observer_of_the_Holy_See_to_the_United_Nations); [WorldAtlas](https://www.worldatlas.com/politics/which-countries-are-not-members-of-the-united-nations.html)). Observers can speak and co-sponsor drafts but cannot vote in the General Assembly ([Wikipedia, UN General Assembly observers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_observers)). Many popular sources therefore add them to reach 195, though observer status is not membership.

Wikipedia's broader list takes a different route. It includes polities that consider themselves sovereign and are recognised by at least one other sovereign state, and it draws its definition of statehood from the **Montevideo Convention of 1933** ([List of sovereign states (mirror)](https://landsurvival.com/schools-wikipedia/wp/l/List_of_sovereign_states.htm)). Under that approach, Kosovo is recognised by **99 UN member states** and by Taiwan ([List of sovereign states (mirror)](https://kolibri.teacherinabox.org.au/modules/en-wikipedia_for_schools-static/wp/l/List_of_sovereign_states.htm)). Taiwan maintains unofficial relations with many states, and **56 states** keep some form of unofficial mission there ([Wikipedia, List of states with limited recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition)). A separate Wikipedia article on the 2020s counts **195 widely recognised sovereign states**, two associated states and **16 states with limited recognition** ([Wikipedia, List of sovereign states in the 2020s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_in_the_2020s)). The sources disagree on how these categories add up. The live page's 205 and the 2020s article's breakdown are not reconciled, and an older mirror's breakdown of 193 plus 10 is outdated. Wikipedia itself states that compiling such a list is contested, because no criteria are binding on all states ([Wikipedia, List of sovereign states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states)).

## Territory-inclusive standards are not country counts

Standards that count territories give much higher figures, and they answer a different question. ISO 3166-1 assigns codes to **249** countries, dependent territories and special areas of geographical interest, including Hong Kong, Puerto Rico and Antarctica ([Wikipedia, ISO 3166-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-1); [IPStack, Country Codes (2026)](https://ipstack.com/country-code/)). The ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 article gives **242 officially assigned codes** plus 7 reassigned ones, which also sums to 249 but uses a different breakdown ([Wikipedia, ISO 3166-1 alpha-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-1_alpha-2)). The CIA World Factbook's live page lists **254 entries**, which it describes as countries and territories ([CIA, Countries](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/countries/)). Its 2023 archive listed **262** ([CIA, 2023 archive](https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/about/archives/2023/countries/)). One third-party successor site says the CIA stopped publishing the Factbook on 4 February 2026 ([WorldFactBooks.com](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/)). That claim rests on a single source, and the CIA's own page was not checked for it.

## Conclusion

For a single answer, use **193**, the UN member-state count. It is official, it has been stable since 2011, and no 2025 or 2026 change alters it. State the convention whenever you give a number, because "195" and "205" are equally common and describe different things. Use 195 when observers are included, and use the broader figures only when the question concerns recognition, territories or statistical coding.

The main uncertainty is the status of contested states such as Kosovo, Taiwan, Western Sahara and Somaliland. None has a UN seat, and their counts depend entirely on the criteria chosen. Bougainville is the one pending case that could eventually change the answer, but its target date of 2030 is years away, and the PNG parliament had not ratified the timeline when the sources were written.
