# Structured literacy front-loads explicit, sequenced code teaching

Structured literacy differs from typical balanced or whole-language practice mainly in what comes first and how explicitly it is taught. Structured literacy explicitly and systematically teaches the structure of written language, starting with sound-symbol relationships and building cumulatively to morphology, syntax and comprehension ([IDA fact sheet](https://dyslexiaida.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_SL-Fact-Sheet.pdf)). Balanced and whole-language practice starts with meaningful texts and treats phonics as one part of a mix, often taught in short lessons or incidentally ([Balanced literacy, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanced_literacy)). The evidence most clearly supports systematic phonics over no-phonics control conditions, with **effects of about d = 0.41 on reading in the 2001 NRP-based meta-analysis** ([Ehri et al. 2001](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Systematic-Phonics-Instruction-Helps-Students-Learn-Ehri-Nunes/fbef17d411d66b90f1a3ae17c0c66a2a0fb208f1)). Direct evidence that structured literacy beats balanced literacy is weaker: a pooled meta-analysis reports larger effects, but its randomised trials show only a small difference and its credibility has been challenged. Policy has moved faster than the research, especially in the US, where **at least 14 states require schools to stop using three-cueing materials** ([EdCircuit, citing Education Week](https://edcircuit.com/science-of-reading-guide/)).

## Structured literacy is defined as an approach, not a programme

The International Dyslexia Association (IDA) gives the most authoritative definition. Its July 2024 working definition describes structured literacy as an integrated approach to reading and writing that is "not a programme," although educators often use one or more programmes to deliver it ([IDA fact sheet](https://dyslexiaida.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_SL-Fact-Sheet.pdf)). The IDA says the term is an umbrella for evidence-based programmes aligned with its Knowledge and Practice Standards ([IDA](https://or.dyslexiaida.org/what-is-structured-literacy-2/)). Its defining features are explicit, systematic, cumulative and diagnostic teaching. Explicit teaching makes clear what students must know and do, and diagnostic teaching uses formal and informal data to decide what to teach next ([IDA fact sheet](https://dyslexiaida.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_SL-Fact-Sheet.pdf)).

Component lists differ. Many sources list six components: phonology, sound-symbol association, syllables, morphology, syntax and semantics ([NCIL](https://improvingliteracy.org/resource/features-of-structured-literacy-instruction/); [Arizona Department of Education](https://www.azed.gov/scienceofreading/structured-literacy)). The IDA's 2024 definition instead lists oral-language domains (phonology, morphology, semantics, syntax and pragmatics) and their written form, orthography ([IDA fact sheet](https://dyslexiaida.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_SL-Fact-Sheet.pdf)). Sequencing is described as simple to complex, but no source gives a fixed order for morphology or syntax. The IDA adds that a planned sequence does not mean there is only one correct one, and that it should be adapted to the learner ([IDA fact sheet](https://dyslexiaida.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_SL-Fact-Sheet.pdf)).

Jurisdictions use the label differently. Texas law defines dyslexia instruction as multisensory structured literacy ([SREB](https://www.sreb.org/resources/state-dyslexia-policies)). New Zealand's Ministry prefers "structured approach to literacy" over the IDA term ([RNZ](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/516423/using-a-structured-literacy-approach-to-teach-reading-what-you-need-to-know)). England's Department for Education (DfE) does not use "structured literacy" as its framing term. It requires systematic synthetic phonics ([DfE reading framework](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/664f600c05e5fe28788fc437/The_reading_framework_.pdf)), which is narrower than the IDA's multi-strand description.

## Balanced and whole-language practice starts with meaning

Whole language holds that children learn language naturally through meaningful texts, and Kenneth Goodman's miscue research framed reading as a "psycholinguistic guessing game" ([NIFDI](https://www.nifdi.org/news/hempenstall-blog/441-part-1-whole-language-what-was-that-all-about)). Balanced literacy added some phonics to that base. Critics say it is in practice usually whole language, a claim that Fountas and Pinnell reject ([Fountas & Pinnell](https://fpblog.fountasandpinnell.com/fact-check-responding-to-misinformation-about-fountas-and-pinnell-literacy); [Ontario Human Rights Commission inquiry](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/sites/default/files/FINAL%20R2R%20REPORT%20DESIGNED%20April%2012.pdf)). The sources for this section are largely encyclopaedic, blog or critic accounts, so the contrast below reflects competing characterisations.

| Dimension | Structured literacy | Balanced or whole-language practice |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | Explicit sound-symbol teaching, then cumulative skills ([IDA fact sheet](https://dyslexiaida.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_SL-Fact-Sheet.pdf)) | Meaningful, whole texts; phonics in mini-lessons or incidentally ([Balanced literacy, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanced_literacy)) |
| Role of context | Not a decoding strategy; decodable texts match taught patterns ([Spear-Swerling slides](https://mlc-wels.edu/openlearning/wp-content/uploads/sites/69/2025/02/Open-Learning-1-Feb-2025-Structured-Literacy-slide-pdf.pdf)) | Predicting words from meaning and pictures, the three-cueing approach ([OHRC](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/en/right-read-inquiry-report/8-curriculum-and-instruction)) |
| Typical lesson | Orton-Gillingham style: phonogram review, drills, new concept, reading, dictation ([PRIDE](https://pridereadingprogram.com/orton-gillingham-lesson-plans/)) | Read-aloud, shared reading, guided reading, independent reading, word study ([K12Reader](https://www.k12reader.com/category/balanced-literacy/)) |
| Assessment | Diagnostic, driving next teaching step ([IDA fact sheet](https://dyslexiaida.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_SL-Fact-Sheet.pdf)) | Leveled texts and running records ([Ontario survey, MDPI](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14070791)) |

Balanced-literacy classrooms typically run a reading workshop: a mini-lesson, guided reading groups, independent reading, conferencing and a closing share ([Scholar Commons](https://scholarcommons.sc.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=5902&context=etd)). Guided reading groups are small, and Fountas and Pinnell give a size of 4 to 8 students ([Fountas & Pinnell](https://fpblog.fountasandpinnell.com/what-is-the-difference-between-guided-reading-and-leveled-literacy-intervention-lli)). Within this model, phonics appears as short lessons, and comprehension and meaning come first. Publishers linked to the balanced model now describe their phonics as systematic, so the difference is narrowing in presentation. The retrieved sources do not show whether classroom practice has changed to match.

## Outcome evidence favours systematic phonics, but the structured-versus-balanced gap is uncertain

The core evidence is strongest for systematic phonics against no-phonics controls. The NRP-based meta-analysis pooled 66 comparisons and found **d = 0.41 for phonics on reading**, with effects lasting after instruction ended ([Ehri et al. 2001](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Systematic-Phonics-Instruction-Helps-Students-Learn-Ehri-Nunes/fbef17d411d66b90f1a3ae17c0c66a2a0fb208f1)). The NRP's own report gives **d = 0.44** for the same question, so the reported figures differ ([NRP report](https://www.thereadingleague.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/NRP-Report.pdf)). Galuschka et al. (2014) found phonics the only approach whose efficacy for reading and spelling was statistically confirmed in children with reading disabilities, with a mean effect of .32 ([Galuschka et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/260448205_Effectiveness_of_Treatment_Approaches_for_Children_and_Adolescents_with_Reading_Disabilities_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Randomized_Controlled_Trials)).

Effects are smaller over time. A 2016 meta-analysis found a short-term effect of **dw = 0.37 that fell to dw = 0.22 at follow-up**, with phonics and fluency interventions tending not to maintain gains ([Suggate 2016](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-Meta-Analysis-of-the-Long-Term-Effects-of-and-Suggate/2d608429283eb111371bfb96d0064b66889604db)). The EEF rates phonics as moderate impact for very low cost, with about four additional months' progress, and says it improves word-reading accuracy but not comprehension ([EEF Phonics toolkit](https://images.scholastic.co.uk/assets/a/5c/7d/eef-phonics-1619193.pdf)).

The comparison with whole language is weaker. A critical reanalysis argues that the NRP and Ehri et al. pooled unsystematic phonics with no-phonics controls, so it cannot show that systematic phonics beats standard alternatives ([Reconsidering the Evidence](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09515-y)). The NRP subanalysis of systematic phonics against whole language used 12 studies, none of them randomised trials, and found d = 0.31 ([Reconsidering the Evidence](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09515-y)). Torgerson et al. (2006) found no clear evidence that synthetic phonics beats analytic phonics ([Torgerson et al. 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265619755_A_Systematic_Review_of_the_Research_Literature_on_the_Use_of_Phonics_in_the_Teaching_of_Reading_and_Spelling)).

The most direct structured-versus-balanced evidence is a meta-analysis of 44 structured and 34 balanced studies. It reports weighted mean effects of **.44 for structured literacy and .33 for balanced literacy** ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/397609538_An_exploratory_quantitative_analysis_of_research_on_balanced_literacy_and_structured_literacy)). A companion version found the randomised-trial difference small, at d = .11 and .15, and its funnel-plot analysis suggested the structured-literacy outcomes may be inflated ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387497935_Structured_Literacy_Compared_to_Balanced_Literacy_A_meta-analysis)). A co-author's summary says both journals raised concerns, including non-systematic programme selection and unclear definitions, and that many programmes were coded identically to the NRP's systematic-phonics and whole-language categories ([Teaching by Science](https://www.teachingbyscience.com/systematic-phonics-instruction)). This summary is not a journal record, and the publication status could not be confirmed. The evidence therefore suggests that structured literacy probably helps, but the size of the advantage over balanced literacy is uncertain.

Recent trials are few. A 2026 cluster-randomised trial in Uganda found **0.244 SD for a structured Luganda literacy package** compared with usual provision, but it did not compare against balanced literacy ([Frontiers in Education](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1945498/full)). A vendor-run Missouri trial of more than 3,500 students is reported only through a commercial blog, and the full report was not located ([LXD Research](https://lxdresearch.com/structured-literacy-scripted-curricula-evidence)).

## Policy and preparation are moving faster than evidence

Policy has shifted most sharply in the US. Education Week's tracker counts **43 states and DC** with laws or policies on evidence-based reading since 2013, while Phonics.org gives 42 states and DC, so the figure depends on the source ([EdCircuit](https://edcircuit.com/science-of-reading-guide/); [Phonics.org](https://cms.phonics.org/science-of-reading-legislation-a-state-by-state-overview/)). Restrictions vary in strength. Ohio's 2024 department guidance defines three-cueing as any model that teaches reading through meaning, structure and syntax, and prohibits it from core curricula unless a waiver is granted ([Ohio Department of Education](https://education.ohio.gov/getattachment/Topics/Learning-in-Ohio/English-Language-Art/Resources-for-English-Language-Arts/High-Quality-Instructional-Materials-in-English-La/Three-Cueing-Guidance-Final-March-2024.pdf.aspx?lang=en-US)). Louisiana's Act 517 prohibits the system ([Louisiana guidance](https://doe.louisiana.gov/docs/default-source/literacy/act-517-three-cueing-system-ban-guidance.pdf?sfvrsn=98016318_0)). Missouri bars three-cueing only as the "primary instructional method" and requires an annual checklist ([Missouri Independent](https://missouriindependent.com/2025/07/11/missouri-lawmakers-ban-controversial-reading-instruction-model-as-primary-method/)). Oklahoma's Senate Bill 362 was reported as one vote from the governor's desk, but its final enactment was not verified ([Oklahoma Policy Institute](https://ocpathink.org/post/independent-journalism/ban-on-three-cueing-in-reading-nears-final-passage)).

Teacher preparation is the main lever. NCTQ's June 2026 review found the share of elementary programmes earning an "A" rose **from 26% in 2023 to 53% in 2026**, but one in five programmes still teach debunked methods ([NCTQ](https://www.nctq.org/research-insights/decoding-progress-in-reading-preparation/)). The National Education Policy Center disputes the methodology, so the 53% figure reflects a contested measure ([NEPC](https://www.nepc.colorado.edu/publication-announcement/2026/08/reading-prep)). In Ontario, the inquiry found that teacher education and additional qualification courses include little on direct, systematic foundational word-reading instruction ([OHRC executive summary](https://www.ohrc.on.ca/sites/default/files/Right%20to%20Read%20Executive%20Summary_OHRC%20English_0.pdf)).

Assessment differs across systems. England's phonics screening check is taken by every Year 1 pupil, and the DfE does not publish school-level results ([Schools Insight](https://schoolsinsight.co.uk/blog/phonics-screening-check.html)). Sources conflict on the 2026 pass mark: one reports 32 out of 40, but a DfE-quoted line gives **31**, which is the figure to use ([Schools Insight](https://schoolsinsight.co.uk/blog/phonics-screening-check.html); [Monster Learning](https://www.monster-learning.com/phonics-screening-check/)). Australia's federal government is investing **$10.8 million** in a voluntary Year 1 phonics check, and states differ ([Australian Government](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-curriculum/year-1-phonics-check)).

Implementation barriers are consistent but poorly measured. Ontario survey respondents named teacher training, resources, funding and teacher beliefs as factors that can help or hinder implementation ([Educational Sciences](https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci14070791)). New Zealand's Budget 2026 includes **$136.9 million operating expenditure over four years** for maths and literacy ([NZ Ministry of Education](https://www.education.govt.nz/our-work/publications/corporate-documents/budget-2026/investing-primary-learning)). No source measured classroom minutes, per-pupil costs or the scale of materials change.

## Conclusion

The practical difference between structured and balanced literacy is clear: structured literacy makes code instruction explicit, sequenced and diagnostic, while balanced practice places meaning and whole-text reading first. The evidence strongly supports systematic phonics over no instruction in phonics, and it supports its benefits for decoding and for children with reading difficulties. It does not yet show that structured literacy beats balanced literacy on comprehension or long-term outcomes. Readers should treat the reported structured-versus-balanced advantage as a hypothesis rather than a settled finding.

Policy and practice are ahead of the trials. Schools and governments are changing curricula, teacher preparation and screening on the basis of a consistent direction of evidence, but the costs, time allocations and classroom-level effects remain unmeasured. Future decisions would benefit from direct randomised comparisons between structured and balanced programmes, from follow-up into the upper primary years, and from transparent reporting of what "balanced" means in each study.
