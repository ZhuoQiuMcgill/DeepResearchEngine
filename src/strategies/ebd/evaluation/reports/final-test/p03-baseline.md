# Everest stands at 8,848.86 metres, snow included

Mount Everest's officially accepted height is **8,848.86 m (29,031.7 ft) above sea level**, a figure that Nepal and China jointly announced on **8 December 2020** ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2020/12/08/it-s-official-mount-everest-is-8-848-86-metres-tall); [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/world/mount-everest-has-grown-taller)). This is a snow-inclusive measurement, taken to the top of the snow and ice. No official rock-only height was published with it, and the Kathmandu Post reports that a rock height has not been announced ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2020/12/08/it-s-official-mount-everest-is-8-848-86-metres-tall)). The figure is still current: a June 2026 guide says it "remains the accepted height as of 2026" ([Shikhar Adventure](https://shikharadventure.com/blog/mount-everest)), and Britannica describes it as "subsequently widely accepted" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mount-Everest)). The sources I reviewed report no change since 2020. For most purposes, 8,849 m is a fair rounding, but the precise official figure is 8,848.86 m.

## The 2020 survey settled a long-running snow-versus-rock dispute

The new height came from a joint survey. Nepal's surveyors reached the summit in May 2019, and a Chinese team did so in 2020 ([phys.org (AFP)](https://phys.org/news/2020-12-china-nepal-everest-bit-higher.html); [VOA News](https://www.voanews.com/a/east-asia-pacific_china-nepal-agree-mt-everests-height/6199311.html)). Al Jazeera attributes the 8,848.86 m figure to China's Xinhua News Agency and describes it as based on trigonometric and GPS measurements ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/12/8/mt-everest-nepal-china-announce-revised-height-at-8848-metres)). A Nepal Survey Department official said the result was processed from both countries' data and is accurate to within centimetres ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2020/12/08/it-s-official-mount-everest-is-8-848-86-metres-tall)). Nepal's 2019 team also measured snow depth on the summit with GPS and satellite equipment ([phys.org (AFP)](https://phys.org/news/2020-12-china-nepal-everest-bit-higher.html)).

The earlier figures differed largely because the two countries measured different things. The Kathmandu Post identifies the disagreement as being over whether to use snow height or rock height ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2020/12/08/it-s-official-mount-everest-is-8-848-86-metres-tall)), and AFP reports that the two countries reportedly agreed in 2010 that their measurements referred to different things ([Malay Mail (AFP)](https://www.malaymail.com/news/life/2020/12/09/everest-grows-as-china-nepal-agree-new-height/1930161)). Nepal launched its survey after suggestions that tectonic movement, including the 2015 earthquake, might have changed the height, and China later joined ([Malay Mail (AFP)](https://www.malaymail.com/news/life/2020/12/09/everest-grows-as-china-nepal-agree-new-height/1930161)). The 2019 Nepali measurement was also the first time Nepal independently measured the peak ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/12/8/mt-everest-nepal-china-announce-revised-height-at-8848-metres)).

| Figure | Origin | What it measured |
|---|---|---|
| 8,840 m | First British determination, around 1856 ([Malay Mail (AFP)](https://www.malaymail.com/news/life/2020/12/09/everest-grows-as-china-nepal-agree-new-height/1930161)) | Not specified |
| 8,848 m (29,028 ft) | Survey of India, trigonometry from Bihar ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2020/12/08/it-s-official-mount-everest-is-8-848-86-metres-tall)) | Snow height, per Al Jazeera ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/12/8/mt-everest-nepal-china-announce-revised-height-at-8848-metres)) |
| 8,844.43 m | China's 2005 measurement, excluding the snow cap ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/world/mount-everest-has-grown-taller); [VOA News](https://www.voanews.com/a/east-asia-pacific_china-nepal-agree-mt-everests-height/6199311.html)) | Rock height |
| 8,848.86 m (29,031.7 ft) | Joint Nepal–China survey, 2020 ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2020/12/08/it-s-official-mount-everest-is-8-848-86-metres-tall)) | Snow-inclusive height |

The sources differ on the Survey of India date, with the Kathmandu Post giving 1954 and another source citing 1955. VOA also gives a figure of about 8,842 m ([VOA News](https://www.voanews.com/a/east-asia-pacific_china-nepal-agree-mt-everests-height/6199311.html)), which does not match the other reported figures. The 8,848.86 m total is therefore best understood as a snow-inclusive figure, and it is about 0.86 m above the long-used 8,848 m. The gap most likely reflects improved measurement rather than a physical change in the summit, though this is an inference from the sources, not a conclusion any of them states.

## Rock height figures need careful sourcing

The most common "rock height" figure, 8,844.43 m (29,017 ft), appears in travel blogs such as [Magical Nepal](https://www.magicalnepal.com/blog/how-tall-is-mount-everest/) and [Index Adventure](https://indexadventure.com/blog/mount-everest-height-in-feet-meters-and-km). Both are secondary sources, but the number matches China's 2005 measurement as reported by Fox News and VOA. No official source gives a rock height from the 2020 survey, so 8,844.43 m should be read as a 2005 Chinese figure, not an official 2020 one. Readers should treat it as the rock-only reference point, and the 8,848.86 m total as the official height.

Several popular pages also claim that Everest is growing about 4 mm per year. These claims do not come from authoritative sources, and I could not verify them, so they should not be relied on.

## Conclusion

For a direct answer, Everest is **8,848.86 m (29,031.7 ft)** above sea level, the official Nepal–China figure from December 2020. That figure includes the snow and ice on the summit. The rock-only figure of 8,844.43 m is a 2005 Chinese measurement and not an official 2020 value. Anyone citing Everest's height should state which of these two figures they mean, because the two differ by more than 4 m.

The main limitation is sourcing. The official figures reach me through news reports quoting the joint announcement, not through a primary Nepal Survey Department or Xinhua document. The reported date of the earlier Survey of India work also varies between sources. Neither point changes the answer, but a reader who needs a formal citation should check the original government announcement. Since no change has been reported since 2020, 8,848.86 m remains the figure to use.
