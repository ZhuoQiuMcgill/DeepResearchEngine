# Darwin Explained Sorting, Not the Origin of Variation

Darwin explained how differences among individuals accumulate and get sorted, but he did not explain what causes each difference. His mechanism was **natural selection acting on individual variations that are useful and inherited**, and he admitted that "our ignorance of the laws of variation is profound," with **not one case in a hundred** where he could assign a reason for a given variation ([Darwin Online, Origin 5th ed.](https://darwin-online.org.uk/content/contentblock?itemID=F387&basepage=1&hitpage=1&viewtype=text)). His attempt to explain inheritance, **pangenesis** in *The Variation of Animals and Plants under Domestication* (1868), he called "merely a provisional hypothesis or speculation" ([Darwin Online, Variation vol. 2](https://darwin-online.org.uk/converted/published/1868_Variation_F877/1868_Variation_F877.2.html)). Historians disagree on how much of an "explanation" this amounts to: some say Darwin explained the process, while others say he "glossed over" heredity ([PNAS 2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2201327119)). After 1900, Mendelian genetics supplied discrete units of inheritance, and the modern account names mutation as the ultimate source of new variation, with recombination and gene flow as further sources. The answer to the question is therefore "yes, but only for sorting," and the source of variation remained unexplained in Darwin's own lifetime.

## What Darwin said caused individual differences

In the 1859 *Origin*, Darwin treated individual differences as a basic fact: no two members of a species are "cast in the very same mould," and these differences are the raw material for selection ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). He insisted the causes were real but unknown. He said "a cause for each must exist," and he rejected the word "chance" as "wholly incorrect," using it only to mark ignorance ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). His one specific hypothesis was that conditions of life act first on the reproductive system. He based this on the effects of confinement and cultivation on breeding, and on "sporting" buds in plants ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). He also observed that seedlings from one fruit can differ even when the parents shared conditions, which he read as showing that the laws of reproduction and inheritance matter more than direct effects of conditions ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)).

Darwin gave environment and use a real but limited role. He thought climate and food have "extremely small" direct effects in animals and perhaps somewhat greater effects in plants ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). He also argued that climate and food cannot by themselves produce the co-adaptations seen in nature ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). He acknowledged that the two causes are hard to separate: "we cannot tell how much to attribute to the accumulative action of natural selection, and how much to the definite action of the conditions of life" ([Sacred Texts, Origin Ch. V](https://www.sacred-texts.com/aor/darwin/origin/or05.htm)). Use and disuse were, in his view, "more potent" in producing constitutional differences than direct climatic effects, with the domestic duck's altered wing and leg bones as an example ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)).

## How selection sorts differences, and the pigeon test case

Selection, in Darwin's account, preserves any variation that is "in any degree profitable" and that is generally inherited ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). Heritability is the decisive condition. In the sixth edition he wrote that a variation that is not inherited is unimportant, and that inheritance should be treated as the rule ([Darwin, Origin (6th ed., 1872), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2009/2009-h/2009-h.htm)). Selection can then diverge lineages: the more varied the descendants, the more places in nature they can occupy, so the most divergent variations tend to be kept ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). Sexual selection added weapons and ornaments, which can pass to male offspring ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)).

Pigeon breeding was Darwin's main analogy. He kept many breeds, argued that all domestic pigeons descend from the rock pigeon, *Columba livia*, and used the reappearance of the blue colour and black wing bars in crossbreeds as evidence of reversion to an ancestral form ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). He described breeders' work as "accumulative selection," and he noted that human selection acts only on visible traits, while nature acts on internal organs and constitution as well ([Darwin, Origin (1859), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1228/1228-h/1228-h.htm)). The argument works by analogy: if breeders can produce strikingly different breeds from one stock, nature could produce species from varieties over longer periods. The analogy depends on the common-ancestry premise, and the sources do not show whether Darwin's pigeon work fed directly into his 1868 inheritance theory.

## Pangenesis, the blending problem, and Darwin's response

In 1868 Darwin proposed that cells throughout the body shed minute particles, gemmules, which gather in the reproductive elements and carry heritable traits ([Darwin, Variation (1868), Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3332/pg3332-images.html)). He argued that variability "depends on at least two distinct groups of causes": the deficiency, superabundance, fusion and transposition of gemmules, and the redevelopment of long-dormant gemmules ([Darwin, Variation (1868), Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Variation_of_Animals_and_Plants_under_Domestication/XXVII)). This linked variation to inheritance, and it allowed changes in body parts to reach the reproductive cells, which is why it is often called neo-Lamarckian ([Wikipedia, Pangenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangenesis)). Darwin never tested it experimentally ([Embryo Project, via Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/10552077/Charles_Darwins_Theory_of_Pangenesis)).

Pangenesis also implied blending inheritance, and this created the main objection of the period. Fleeming Jenkin's 1867 review argued that a beneficial variety would be diluted in a few generations, so selection could not establish it ([PNAS, Mendel and Darwin](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2122144119)). The classical account holds that Jenkin's "swamping" argument was the leading challenge, and that Darwin answered by giving more weight to use and disuse and to the environment, while downplaying "single variations" ([NCSE, Excursion Ch. 2](https://ncse.ngo/excursion-chapter-2-genetics-and-evolution)). Darwin reported that this objection "gave him more trouble than any other" ([Memorial University review page](https://www.mun.ca/biology/scarr/Fleming_Jenkin_review_1867.html)). A 2024 reassessment disputes this reading: its author argues that the swamping themes are "strikingly absent from Jenkin's text," that Darwin was "probably mostly unimpressed," and that the real problems concerned intercrossing and reversion ([Springer, J Hist Biol 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10739-024-09770-y); [PubMed, Hoquet 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38656677/)). A Cambridge study argues that the swamping argument fails once a flaw in Jenkin's model is removed, provided the mutation rate is high enough ([Cambridge, BJHS](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-for-the-history-of-science/article/abs/did-jenkins-swamping-argument-invalidate-darwins-theory-of-natural-selection/263EE376F7735A474F23DBBD503A363B)). The two accounts disagree on how decisive the critique was, and the sources do not settle it.

Pangenesis itself was widely rejected. When first published it was "almost uniformly rejected," and for a long time it was regarded as "Darwin's biggest mistake" ([PubMed, Criticisms of Pangenesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30037392/)). Galton's blood-transfusion experiments were taken to disprove it; Darwin replied that he had never mentioned blood in his writings ([Wikipedia, Lamarckism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamarckism)). A 2018 review argues that new findings on cell-free DNA, mobile RNAs and inherited acquired characters make it improper to call pangenesis Darwin's biggest mistake ([PubMed, Criticisms of Pangenesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30037392/)). That is an advocacy reading that uses evidence unavailable to Darwin, so it does not settle what Darwin himself believed.

## From Mendel to the modern account of variation

Darwin's influence on Mendel is better documented than the reverse. A 2016 *Genetics* paper states there is "compelling evidence that Darwin knew nothing of Mendel then, or at any time of his life," while Mendel acquired a copy of the *Origin* in 1863 and probably read it ([Genetics, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1534/genetics.116.194613)). A 2019 article reports that Focke's 1881 book, which summarised Mendel's experiments, was in Darwin's library, but "the pages of these summaries remain uncut" ([Nature, Mendel and Darwin](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41437-019-0289-9)). A 2022 PNAS article says Darwin had access to a published account that mentioned Mendel's work, though whether he read it is not established ([PNAS, Mendel and Darwin](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2122144119)). The weight of evidence is that Darwin never engaged with Mendel's paper.

Mendelian results, rediscovered in 1900 by Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns and Erich von Tschermak, showed that traits are carried by discrete units that can be masked and passed on intact ([Oregon State OER](https://open.oregonstate.education/cultivatedplants/chapter/genetics/); [ScienceInsights](https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-the-blending-theory-of-inheritance-explained/)). Pangenesis was then replaced by germ-plasm and chromosomal theories, and gemmules by genes ([ASU Embryo Project](https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/charles-darwins-theory-pangenesis)). Ronald Fisher showed in 1918 that continuous variation could arise from many discrete loci ([Wikidoc](https://www.wikidoc.org/index.php/Modern_evolutionary_synthesis)), and Dobzhansky's 1937 *Genetics and the Origin of Species* is often treated as the start of the modern synthesis, which tied selection to genetic variation ([Social Sci LibreTexts](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Anthropology/Biological_Anthropology/Book:_Biological_Anthropology_(Saneda_and_Field)/I:_Evolutionary_Theory/1.7:_Modern_Synthesis)). Dobzhansky's work on wild populations showed large amounts of genetic diversity ([Wikipedia, Population genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_genetics)).

Current teaching names **mutation as the ultimate source of new variation**, with crossing over, independent assortment, random fertilisation and gene flow as further sources ([Albert.io](https://www.albert.io/blog/genetic-diversity-ap-biology-review/); [CK-12](https://flexbooks.ck12.org/cbook/ck-12-advanced-biology/section/10.34/primary/lesson/mutation-and-gene-flow-advanced-bio-adv/)). The figures differ by organism and measure, as the table shows.

| Measure | Reported value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| New mutations per human generation | About 64 | [Wikipedia, Mutation rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation_rate) |
| Human mutation rate, 2015 estimate | 1.61 ± 0.13 × 10⁻⁸ per base per generation | [PLoS Genetics](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pgen.1005550) |
| Recombination events per generation | About 35 in total, about one per chromosome | [UCSD Personal Genomics textbook](http://admixgenomics.ucsd.edu/personal-genomics-textbook/_sources/ancestry/popgen/recombination.md) |

The table shows that recombination can outweigh mutation in generating novel genomes in some bacteria, while mutation is the only source of new alleles. Mutation and recombination supply the raw material, so selection's role is to sort it. A 2015 *Genetics* paper found that once standing variation is depleted, adaptation proceeds mostly by new mutations, and that faster environmental change depletes standing variation more quickly ([Genetics, Catch Me if You Can](https://dx.doi.org/10.1534/genetics.115.178574)). A 2026 bioRxiv preprint reports that standing variation was the main driver of some polygenic shifts, but this has not been peer-reviewed ([bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.14.751342v1.full)).

## Where historians still disagree

Historians largely agree that Darwin succeeded in explaining branching and selection but could not convincingly explain the origin and maintenance of variation ([SciELO](http://www.scielo.br/j/gmb/a/bjgRXLDWH7bjfnQMJx3qVjK/?lang=en)). They disagree about three things. The first is whether Darwin was a blending theorist: a historian's review argues that Darwin's own evidence did not support blending in any simple way ([MPIWG preprint 294](https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/Preprints/P294.PDF)), while Wikipedia treats blending as the standard reading. The second is whether pangenesis was a mistake or a response to critics: a 2014 reception study argues it answered critics of selection ([Springer, Hist Biol](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10739-014-9377-0)), while a 2018 chapter argues it influenced de Vries, Weismann and Galton ([PubMed, Influence of Pangenesis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30037393/)). The third is whether the modern synthesis narrowed variation to genetics. The extended synthesis argues that developmental processes share responsibility for the origin of character variation ([Royal Society](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rspb.2015.1019)). Critics of this view note that Haldane and Dobzhansky discussed mutation bias early on ([ScienceDirect, Modern Synthesis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/social-sciences/modern-synthesis)).

## Conclusion

For a general reader, the simplest accurate answer is that Darwin explained why differences accumulate, not why each one arises. Natural selection sorts variations that are useful and inherited, and Darwin was candid that the causes of most variation were unknown. His attempt to fill the inheritance gap, pangenesis, was speculative, implied blending, and was largely abandoned after Mendel's units were rediscovered in 1900. The modern account keeps Darwin's sorting logic and fills the gap he left with mutation, recombination and gene flow.

For a university study, the claim that Darwin "explained" differences needs to specify the standard being applied. Under a causal standard of origin, he did not explain variation. Under a standard of how differences accumulate, his account remains central. The disputes over Jenkin's critique, the Lamarckian content of pangenesis and the reach of the modern synthesis are all open, and readers should check the primary texts and the paywalled 2024 reassessment before relying on any one side.
