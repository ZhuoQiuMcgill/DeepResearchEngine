# Nationality decides whether a one-week UK trip needs permission

For most travellers, the answer is yes, though the type of permission depends on nationality. British and Irish citizens need no visa or ETA, and Irish citizens must carry a valid Irish passport when travelling from outside the Common Travel Area ([Ireland.ie](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/overseas-travel/advice/great-britain/)). Most other visa-exempt nationalities, including the US, Canada, Australia and EU/EEA states, need a **£20 Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA)**, which the Home Office rolled out by nationality from 2023 to 2025 ([Home Office ETA guidance v9.0](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68dbeed3dadf7616351e4c54/Electronic+Travel+Authorisation+Guidance.pdf); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/the-uk-electronic-travel-authorisation-scheme-challenges-solutions-and-recommendations)). Nationals on the Home Office visa-national list, such as India, Pakistan, Nigeria, China and Turkey, need a **Standard Visitor visa costing £135**, which requires an online application and biometric enrolment ([Home Office visa poster](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69de095b2b60817d99ff8ddd/UK_Visa_requirements_April_2026.pdf); [findmyvisa.co.uk](https://findmyvisa.co.uk/visitor-visa)). The passport must stay valid for the whole stay, and holding permission does not guarantee entry. Because country lists change, as Jordan's move to the visa list in September 2024 showed, travellers should confirm their status on the official checker before booking ([Home Office ETA guidance v9.0](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68dbeed3dadf7616351e4c54/Electronic+Travel+Authorisation+Guidance.pdf)). Rules are stated as of 20 November 2026.

## Which permission applies depends on passport nationality

The ETA route is the simplest for most tourists. Sources say that **85 countries** must hold an ETA ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/07/the-uk-electronic-travel-authorisation-scheme-challenges-solutions-and-recommendations)), but the official ETA national list itself is only referenced in the Home Office guidance and was not reproduced there, so the total is not confirmed against an official source ([Home Office ETA guidance v9.0](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68dbeed3dadf7616351e4c54/Electronic+Travel+Authorisation+Guidance.pdf)). The visa-national list is more reliable because it appears on an official Home Office poster, though that poster is primarily a transit document and states that ETA-eligible countries are not listed there ([Home Office visa poster](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69de095b2b60817d99ff8ddd/UK_Visa_requirements_April_2026.pdf)). Dual nationals should travel on their British passport, since the US Embassy notes that dual citizens must enter on a UK passport and cannot obtain an ETA on a foreign one ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/united-kingdom.html)).

## Application costs, timing and documents

For an ETA, the **£20 fee** is paid online and covers each traveller, including babies, who needs an individual authorisation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_(United_Kingdom))). The sources disagree on when the increase took effect. Arletti Partners says the fee rose from £16 to £20 on 8 April 2026 ([Arletti Partners](https://arlettipartners.com/uk-eta-cost-to-rise-from-april-2026/)), while visaeta.uk gives 25 February 2026 in one guide and 8 April 2026 in another ([visaeta.uk](https://visaeta.uk/blog/uk-eta-fee-increase-20-pounds-2026-guide/); [visaeta.uk](https://visaeta.uk/blog/uk-eta-fee-increase-2026/)). The 8 April date is more consistent across sources, though no official GOV.UK fee page was confirmed. Decision timing is also disputed: the Wikipedia summary of GOV.UK guidance says a decision usually comes within three working days, while commercial guides say minutes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_(United_Kingdom)); [VisaMet](https://visamet.com/guides/uk-eta-electronic-travel-authorisation-guide-2026)). The three-working-day figure is the safer planning assumption, since it is the more authoritative source. An ETA is valid for two years or until the linked passport expires, so a new passport means a new ETA ([trip1](https://trip1.com/help/visa/visa-info/what-is-the-uk-eta-who-needs-it-cost-and-how-long-it-lasts)).

The visa route is slower and more involved. Applicants apply online on GOV.UK up to three months ahead and then enrol biometrics at a visa application centre ([Connaught Law](https://connaughtlaw.com/standard-visitor-visa-uk-complete-guide/); [visas.guide](https://visas.guide/uk-visit-visa-guide)). The standard decision time is **about three weeks**, but sources differ on when the clock starts. Whytecroft Ford says it starts when the Home Office receives biometric information ([Whytecroft Ford](https://www.whytecroftford.com/uk-immigration/guides/uk-visa-processing-times/)), and the fee is also disputed: visas.guide gives £135 for six months ([visas.guide](https://visas.guide/uk-visit-visa-guide)), while Wesbridge gives £115 ([Wesbridge](https://www.wesbridgeassociates.co.uk/blog/uk-standard-visitor-visa-eligibility-and-rules)). Because the £135 figure matches the rise reported on 8 April 2026, it is the one to use, but applicants should check the Home Office fee calculator for their country.

## Passport validity: the six-month dispute

Sources disagree on whether a passport needs six months' validity. The US Embassy and VisitBritain both say the passport must be valid for the duration of the stay ([travel.state.gov](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/united-kingdom.html); [VisitBritain](https://www.visitbritain.com/en/plan-your-trip/visa-and-immigration-information)). Davidson Morris and mybritishpassport.com also say there is no six-month rule for entry, only validity for the stay ([davidsonmorris.com](https://www.davidsonmorris.com/what-is-the-minimum-passport-validity-to-enter-uk/); [mybritishpassport.com](https://mybritishpassport.com/can-i-enter-the-uk-with-a-passport-about-to-expire/)). Wembley Solicitors says a visitor visa passport must have six months' validity, but it cites no official source ([Wembley Solicitors](https://wembleysolicitors.com/uk-visit-visa-document-checklist)). This report follows the duration-of-stay rule, because two official-style sources and two commercial guides agree, and the six-month claim is unsupported. A one-week traveller should still ensure the passport is valid for the trip and beyond the return date, because airlines may apply stricter rules than the UK border ([visaeta.uk](https://visaeta.uk/blog/passport-validity-for-travel-to-uk-in-2026-what-travellers-must-check-before-departure/)).

## What a one-week visit may cover, and where it ends

A standard visitor can attend meetings, conferences, interviews and training, visit family or friends, and take part in tourism ([visa-applications.org](https://www.visa-applications.org/uk/resources/visitor-visas/standard-visitor-visa)). Paid work for a UK organisation is prohibited, and the rules on paid engagements are narrow ([visa-applications.org](https://www.visa-applications.org/uk/resources/permitted-activities); [Sterling & Wells](https://sterlingandwells.com/blogs/uk-standard-visitor-visa/)). Transit is a common trap. Passengers who pass UK passport control, even briefly, need an ETA if their nationality requires one, and visa nationals need a transit visa too ([Weightmans](https://www.weightmans.com/insights/transit-visas/)). The Home Office says the airside exemption for connecting passengers applies at Heathrow and Manchester and is temporary, though some sources contest which airports it covers ([Home Office ETA factsheet](https://homeofficemedia.blog.gov.uk/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-factsheet-april-2026/); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2025/01/21/uk-drops-eta-requirement-for-airside-transit-passengers)).

The Crown Dependencies are a further change. Since 23 April 2026, eligible visitors travelling directly to Jersey, Guernsey or the Isle of Man from outside the Common Travel Area need an ETA, which must be applied for at least three working days before travel ([Government of Jersey](https://www.gov.je/News/2026/pages/introduction-of-eta-for-non-visa-nationals-travelling-to-jersey--.aspx)). A single ETA covers England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, so a visitor needs no second application for those nations ([Home Office ETA factsheet](https://homeofficemedia.blog.gov.uk/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-factsheet-april-2026/)). Sources disagree on whether the islands run separate permission schemes, and no official page on that point was confirmed ([uk-eta.com](https://uk-eta.com/crown-dependencies-to-open-eta-applications-on-9-april-for-direct-trips-starting-23-april/)).

## Border refusal and carrier enforcement

Holding an ETA or visa does not guarantee entry. The Home Office says an ETA is permission to travel, not a right to enter, and Border Force makes the final decision ([Home Office ETA factsheet](https://homeofficemedia.blog.gov.uk/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-factsheet-april-2026/)). Visitor guidance lists refusal codes for insufficient funds, failure to show a genuine visit, unsatisfactory study arrangements and lack of a required visa ([Home Office Visit guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68e3dbcbc487360cc70ca17a/Visit.pdf)). A one-week visitor's realistic risks are therefore weak evidence of purpose, funds, accommodation or onward travel, and any prior immigration problems. In practice, the first refusal may come at the gate. The Home Office says carriers will prevent boarding without a valid ETA, and ITV reported that some airlines' ground staff are obliged to deny boarding where one is missing ([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/us-travel-advisory-highlights-rising-uk-entry-refusals-for-americans/); [ITV News](https://www.itv.com/news/2026-02-26/airlines-tighten-eta-checks-as-new-border-rules-come-into-force)). Enforcement began in late February 2026, though sources give 25 and 26 February ([VisaHQ](https://www.visahq.com/news/2026-02-26/ae/uk-eta-enforcement-begins-airlines-now-refuse-boarding-to-uae-travellers-without-digital-authorisation/)). Carriers have also wrongly denied boarding to Crown Dependency residents who were exempt ([VisaHQ](https://www.visahq.com/news/2026-04-09/gb/channel-islanders-denied-boarding-abroad-as-airlines-misapply-uk-eta-rules/)), so travellers should carry their documents.

## Conclusion

For a one-week trip, the answer depends on nationality and the route. British and Irish citizens need no permission, most other visa-exempt travellers need a £20 ETA applied for well before departure, and visa nationals need a £135 Standard Visitor visa that takes about three weeks after biometrics. The main uncertainties are the exact ETA country list, the fee start date and the boarding practices of airlines. These should be checked on the official checker and GOV.UK before booking.

The practical implication is that the decisive steps come before departure. Travellers should check their passport nationality, confirm the current fee and passport validity, and plan transit carefully, because passing UK passport control triggers the ETA requirement. A one-week visit that stays within the visitor rules is straightforward, but a poorly documented trip is the most likely source of problems at the border.
