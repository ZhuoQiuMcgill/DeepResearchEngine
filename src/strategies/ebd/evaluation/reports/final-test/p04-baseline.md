# Nationality Decides Whether a UK Week Needs Paperwork

For a one-week trip, the answer depends on your nationality. British and Irish citizens need nothing beyond a valid passport. Nationals of about 83 visa-exempt countries need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA), which costs **£20 per applicant** and is valid for two years. Nationals of the listed visa countries need a **Standard Visitor visa costing £135**, and the published standard processing time is about three weeks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_(United_Kingdom)); [Forsters](https://www.forsters.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Upcoming-changes-to-Home-Office-fees-from-8-April-2026.pdf); [Wesbridge-style summaries aside, the Home Office poster](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69de095b2b60817d99ff8ddd/UK_Visa_requirements_April_2026.pdf)). A stay of up to six months per visit covers a week easily, so the real decision is which document route applies. The sources disagree on the exact list of eligible countries, so the official GOV.UK nationality checker should be used before booking. Any traveller who needs a visa should apply early, because the standard processing time is tight for a short trip.

## Some travellers need no document at all

Several groups are exempt from the ETA. The Home Office states that **Irish citizens do not need an ETA, visa or other immigration permission** under the Common Travel Area, and the Irish government adds that Irish citizens, including dual citizens, do not need a visa when coming from outside it ([Home Office ETA factsheet](https://homeofficemedia.blog.gov.uk/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-factsheet-april-2026/); [Irish Government](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/overseas-travel/advice/great-britain/)). The exemption depends on citizenship, not trip length, so a week's trip raises no separate issue. Irish citizens subject to a deportation order, exclusion order or international travel ban are excluded ([Home Office ETA guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68dbeed3dadf7616351e4c54/Electronic+Travel+Authorisation+Guidance.pdf)). Irish travellers should carry a document showing citizenship, because from 25 February 2026 airlines require a valid Irish passport or passport card for travel between Ireland and the UK, and ferries are subject to the same requirement from 28 September 2026 ([Irish Government](https://www.ireland.ie/en/dfa/overseas-travel/advice/great-britain/)).

British Overseas Territories citizens are also generally exempt when they travel on a British Citizen passport or a BOTC passport. The exemption rests on a 2023 statement relayed by the Bermuda government, and no current GOV.UK page was found to confirm it ([Bernews](https://bernews.com/2023/11/uk-territories-citizens-exempt-from-uk-eta/)). Those who are BOTCs but not British citizens remain subject to immigration control on arrival, so they should check their status before travel. Dual British citizens must enter on a valid UK passport ([US State Department](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/international-travel/International-Travel-Country-Information-Pages/IsleOfMan.html)). Anyone already holding a valid UK visa or settled status generally does not need an ETA ([ETA guide](https://etagov.uk/who-needs-a-uk-eta/)).

## Visa-exempt nationals need an ETA, and the list is disputed

Most visa-exempt nationals need an ETA. Sources give different totals: one guide counts **84 countries** including the United States, Canada, Australia, Japan and most EU states ([VisasNews](https://visasnews.com/en/uk-eta-now-mandatory-for-all-visa-exempt-travelers/)), while a more recent guide says the Immigration Rules appendix naming **83 nationalities** is the only authoritative list ([VisaVerge](https://www.visaverge.com/visa/uk-electronic-travel-authorisation-how-to-apply-and-what-to-know/)). Another source gives 85 ([Morgan Smith Immigration](https://morgansmithimmigration.com/uk-mandatory-electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-february-2026/)). The 84-country list predates March 2026, when Clark Hill reports that **Nicaragua and St Lucia moved to the visa list** ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/united-kingdom-statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-announced/)). Wikipedia also records that Trinidad and Tobago became ineligible in March 2025 and Botswana in October 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_(United_Kingdom))). The most recent and specific evidence therefore points to a list of about 83 countries, but the official GOV.UK checker is the only authoritative answer for a given passport.

The ETA process is straightforward. Applicants pay by credit or debit card, Apple Pay or Google Pay, and apply through the UK ETA app or GOV.UK ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_(United_Kingdom))). Each traveller, including babies and children, needs an individual ETA ([Carter Thomas](https://www.carterthomas.co.uk/a-guide-to-the-electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-scheme/)). The application requires a photo of the passport, a face scan (except for children aged nine or younger), and answers to security questions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_(United_Kingdom))). Decisions usually come within minutes, but **Richmond Chambers advises allowing up to three working days** ([Richmond Chambers](https://immigrationbarrister.co.uk/full-uk-eta-enforcement-announced-for-february-2026/)). The ETA does not guarantee entry, and a refusal carries no right of appeal, though the traveller may apply for a visa instead ([Richmond Chambers](https://immigrationbarrister.co.uk/full-uk-eta-enforcement-announced-for-february-2026/)). Unofficial application websites charge extra and carry scam risks, so the GOV.UK route is the safer option ([GovtSchemes](https://govtschemes.org/uk-travel-rule-change-2026-eta-fees-increased-by-25-percent/)).

## Visa nationals need a Standard Visitor visa

Nationals of countries not on the ETA list must apply for a visa. The Home Office's April 2026 "UK Visa Requirements" poster, last updated in August 2026, lists countries whose nationals need a visa to enter the UK, including Bangladesh, China, India, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines, South Africa, Thailand, Turkey, Ukraine and Vietnam ([Home Office poster](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69de095b2b60817d99ff8ddd/UK_Visa_requirements_April_2026.pdf)). Secondary guides put the number of visa nationals at around 103, but no source confirmed that count against the official list ([VisaETA.uk](https://visaeta.uk/blog/uk-eta-vs-visa/uk-tourist-visa-fees-everything-to-know-in-2026/)).

| Route | Who it applies to | Cost | Timing |
|---|---|---|---|
| No document | British, Irish and exempt groups | Nothing beyond travel costs | None |
| ETA | Visa-exempt nationals (about 83 countries) | £20 per person | Usually minutes; allow up to three working days ([Richmond Chambers](https://immigrationbarrister.co.uk/full-uk-eta-enforcement-announced-for-february-2026/)) |
| Standard Visitor visa | Nationals on the visa list | £135 for up to six months ([Forsters](https://www.forsters.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Upcoming-changes-to-Home-Office-fees-from-8-April-2026.pdf)) | About three weeks from biometrics ([Withrowan](https://withrowan.co.uk/guides/visitor-visa-processing-time)) |

The visa fee is the same for all nationalities ([UK Visa Blog](https://ukvisa.blog/uk-visitor-visa-fees/)) and is not refunded on refusal ([VisaETA.uk](https://visaeta.uk/blog/uk-eta-vs-visa/uk-tourist-visa-fees-everything-to-know-in-2026/)). No Immigration Health Surcharge is payable for a visit visa of six months or less applied for from outside the UK ([Withrowan](https://withrowan.co.uk/guides/immigration-health-surcharge)). The application is made online, with the visa application centre used for biometrics and passport submission ([Mgbe Legal](https://mgbelegal.com/blog/uk-standard-visitor-visa-guide/)). It can be submitted up to three months before travel ([Withrowan](https://withrowan.co.uk/guides/visitor-visa-processing-time)). Applicants must show a genuine visit, an intention to leave, and the means to fund the trip without working or using public funds ([UK Immigration Law](https://www.ukimmigration.law/uk-immigration/uk-visitor-visas/standard-visitor-visa/)). Guides also list accommodation details, an itinerary and proof of ties to home ([Wembley Solicitors](https://wembleysolicitors.com/uk-visit-visa-document-checklist)). Bank statement requirements conflict: one guide suggests about six months of statements, while another suggests money held for at least 28 days ([Wembley Solicitors](https://wembleysolicitors.com/uk-visit-visa-document-checklist); [Moeen & Co.](https://www.moeenco.com/standard-visitor-visa-uk)). The official GOV.UK document list should settle this.

## What a week in the UK allows, and what the border checks

Whichever route applies, a short visit has the same activity limits. Visitors may attend meetings, conferences and job interviews, and may give one-off talks that are not commercial events ([Immigration Rules Appendix Visitor](https://s3.amazonaws.com/thegovernmentsays-files/content/167/1678877.html); [Home Office Visit guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/673351/Visit-guidance-v7.0EXT.PDF)). Remote work for an overseas employer is allowed if it is not the main purpose of the visit ([Rowan](https://withrowan.co.uk/guides/visitor-visa-work-restrictions)). Taking paid work, self-employment or direct selling in the UK is barred, except for a narrow paid engagement that must be completed within 30 days of entry ([Rowan](https://withrowan.co.uk/guides/visitor-visa-work-restrictions)). Marriage, civil partnership and living in the UK through repeated visits are not permitted ([Visa Applications](https://www.visa-applications.org/uk/resources/permitted-activities)).

The passport must be valid for the whole intended stay, and the UK sets no six-month validity margin for visitors, though airlines may refuse boarding if less than three months remain ([infouk.org](https://infouk.org/uk-entry-requirements/)). The ETA is linked to the passport it was issued against, so a new passport means a new ETA ([ETA UK guide](https://etavisauk.org/en/is-my-passport-valid-2026-guide/)). Since 25 February 2026, carriers refuse boarding where an ETA or eVisa is not shown ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/02/uk-immigration-goes-digital-key-changes-commencing-in-february-2026)), so the check happens before departure rather than at the border. Transit passengers who pass UK passport control on a connecting flight need an ETA, while those transiting Heathrow and Manchester without passing passport control do not currently need one ([Home Office ETA factsheet](https://homeofficemedia.blog.gov.uk/electronic-travel-authorisation-eta-factsheet-april-2026/)). The EU's Entry/Exit System is a Schengen system and does not apply to UK entry ([VisaVerge](https://www.visaverge.com/travel/how-etias-and-uk-eta-systems-affect-us-citizens-planning-multi-country-trips/)).

## Fees and eligibility are still moving

Sources disagree on when the ETA fee rose to £20. Wikipedia and VisaVerge give **8 April 2026**, and the Home Office fee table is cited as corroboration ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_Travel_Authorisation_(United_Kingdom)); [VisaVerge](https://www.visaverge.com/travel/uk-raised-electronic-travel-authorisation-fee-by-25-to-20-on-april-8/)). One guide gives 25 February 2026 ([VisaETA.uk](https://visaeta.uk/blog/uk-eta-fee-increase-20-pounds-2026-guide/)). Earlier January 2026 reports describe the fee as £16 with a planned rise to £20 and no date ([Business Travel News Europe](https://www.businesstravelnewseurope.com/Management/UK-government-plans-further-25-per-cent-rise-in-ETA-fee)). I have taken the April 2026 date as correct because it is the most recent and the most widely reported, but travellers should confirm the amount shown on GOV.UK when they apply.

Further changes are possible. A March 2026 Statement of Changes includes visitor-related changes phased in during 2026, and one timeline lists "visitor activity changes (Oct 2026)" without detail ([Adam Bernards](https://adambernards.co.uk/blog/uk-immigration-rule-changes-2026-the-complete-timeline/)). Reported 2027 changes so far concern work, study and settlement routes rather than visits ([Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10267/)). No announced change to the £135 visa fee or the three-week standard was found.

## Conclusion

For most people, a one-week UK trip needs either an ETA or a Standard Visitor visa, and nationality determines which. British and Irish citizens, and some other exempt groups, need no document beyond a valid passport and proof of status. Visa-exempt nationals should apply for the £20 ETA well ahead of travel, since boarding can be refused without one. Nationals on the visa list must apply for a £135 Standard Visitor visa and should allow at least several weeks, because the three-week standard leaves little margin for a short trip.

Because eligibility lists have changed several times since 2025, the official GOV.UK nationality checker should be used close to departure rather than older guides. Travellers should also keep the trip within the visitor rules: a week's tourism, family visits or meetings fits, but paid work does not. Anyone with a passport that expires during the trip, or with British Overseas Territories or dual nationality, should check the position specifically before booking.
