# Environment-Based Design Starts With Context

Environment-Based Design (EBD) is a design methodology in which a designer creates an artifact to change the environment in which it will operate; “environment” means the surrounding system of components and relationships, not just nature or ecology ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf); [Alexandr Moroz, “Environment-Based Design of Software”](https://spectrum.library.concordia.ca/id/eprint/7194/1/Moroz_MASc_S2011.pdf)). Its core activities are environment analysis, conflict identification, and solution generation, repeated as each solution changes the context for the next design round ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf)). In practice, the environment is mapped by identifying relevant components and their relationships, then identifying undesired conflicts; the sources do not prescribe a universal quantitative measure or a single stopping test beyond resolving those conflicts ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf)). EBD also abbreviates Evidence-Based Design, a distinct approach that uses credible research to guide decisions and evaluate outcomes ([The Center for Health Design, “About EBD”](https://www.healthdesign.org/certification-outreach/edac/about-ebd)). Environment-Based Design is therefore best understood as a framework for analyzing and changing a product’s wider context, rather than as a synonym for evidence-led design or ecological design. The approach is associated with Yong Zeng; a Concordia thesis says he introduced it in 2004, drawing on his Axiomatic Theory of Design Modeling ([Alexandr Moroz, “Environment-Based Design of Software”](https://spectrum.library.concordia.ca/id/eprint/7194/1/Moroz_MASc_S2011.pdf)).

## EBD treats design as changing a product’s environment

Zeng defines Environment-Based Design as a step-by-step process for changing an existing environment toward a desired one: design starts from the environment, serves it, and changes it through an artifact ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf)). The artifact must meet human goals and requirements while obeying the environment’s laws and rules; needs or inspiration can come from existing human, natural, and built environments ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf)). His later account describes the method as a recursive evolution of design problems, knowledge, and solutions, organized around environment analysis, conflict identification, and solution generation ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD): a Methodology for Transdisciplinary Design”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/jid-2015-0004)). The environment is thus not merely a backdrop: it supplies requirements and constraints, and it is itself altered by the design.

## Three activities drive an iterative design cycle

Environment analysis identifies the system in which the product will work, including its components and their relationships; conflict identification finds undesired conflicts among those relationships; solution generation proposes a design response ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf)). The cycle then repeats: a new solution becomes part of the environment to be analyzed in the next round, and the process continues until no undesired conflict remains ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf)). A Concordia thesis presents a process diagram with an additional front-end stage, design-problem formulation, taking the product system, customer, and designer as inputs before the three core activities ([Alexandr Moroz, “Environment-Based Design of Software”](https://spectrum.library.concordia.ca/id/eprint/7194/1/Moroz_MASc_S2011.pdf)). That added stage is one documented representation, not a requirement established for every EBD application; likewise, the sources do not specify one universal way to decide whether all undesired conflicts have been resolved.

## The environment includes human, natural, and built dimensions

Zeng’s roadmap classifies the product’s environment as human, natural, and built, and considers it across product lifecycle events ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf)). A Concordia thesis explains these categories as human users, natural laws, and human-created artifacts, while emphasizing relationships among environmental components and between those components and the product ([Shu, Concordia MASc thesis](https://spectrum.library.concordia.ca/id/eprint/982797/1/Shu_MASc_F2017.pdf)). In a formal description, the product system comprises the product and its environment, their interactions, and interactions within each of those two parts ([Alexandr Moroz, “Environment-Based Design of Software”](https://spectrum.library.concordia.ca/id/eprint/7194/1/Moroz_MASc_S2011.pdf)). Process diagrams explicitly include the customer and designer, but the broader human dimension is not a fixed stakeholder roster; the sources do not provide an exhaustive list of stakeholders or establish that these three categories cover every possible classification.

## Environment-Based Design differs from two similarly named approaches

Evidence-Based Design also uses the abbreviation EBD, but it means making built-environment decisions based on credible research to achieve the best possible outcomes ([The Center for Health Design, “About EBD”](https://www.healthdesign.org/certification-outreach/edac/about-ebd)). Its process emphasizes interpreting evidence, developing concepts and hypotheses, collecting baseline measures, monitoring implementation, and measuring post-occupancy results ([The Center for Health Design, “About EBD”](https://www.healthdesign.org/certification-outreach/edac/about-ebd)). By contrast, Environment-Based Design is defined by mapping a product’s environment and using conflicts in that system to drive iterative change; this distinction does not mean it cannot use research evidence ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf); [The Center for Health Design, “About EBD”](https://www.healthdesign.org/certification-outreach/edac/about-ebd)).

Environment-Based Design is also broader than Design for Environment (DfE). The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency describes DfE as integrating environmental considerations into product and service decisions, including lifecycle impacts from raw-material acquisition through disposal or reuse ([U.S. EPA, “Environmental Management Systems: An Implementation Guide”](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-07/documents/ems_an-implementation-guide-for-small-and-medium-sized-organizations_2nded.pdf)). Because Environment-Based Design includes human, built, and natural contexts, it is not inherently a method focused only on reducing ecological impacts ([Yong Zeng, “Environment-Based Design (EBD)”](https://users.encs.concordia.ca/~design/reference/EBD-Final.pdf)). The EPA comparison describes DfE specifically and should not be taken to define every environmentally focused design approach.

## Conclusion

Environment-Based Design is most useful as a way to frame design problems relationally: identify the system around a product, locate conflicts in that system, and test solutions by examining how they reshape the context. Its distinctive contribution is this iterative environment-centered logic, not a prescribed quantitative score or a universal stakeholder checklist.

Because EBD is also widely used for Evidence-Based Design, spelling out the full name avoids ambiguity. The available definitions support a clear conceptual distinction, but leave room for variation in how teams formulate the initial problem, map stakeholders, and judge when conflicts have been sufficiently resolved.
